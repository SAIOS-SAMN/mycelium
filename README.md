# mycelium

A distributed mesh network for **POW++**, a family of proofs of work in
which the work proven is verified computation rather than hash search. The
network's nodes are **hyphae** (courts), which adjudicate, settle and keep
the ledger, and **minds** (clients), which perceive tasks, learn a shared
language, and submit work for adjudication. Every quantity the protocol
decides on is computed exactly, in the conformal geometric algebra
Cl(4,1) over the rationals ℚ, with no floating point anywhere.

> **Status: research prototype, published for peer review.** The code
> compiles and its scoped test suites pass. The development deployment has
> an open regression (see [Known issues](#known-issues)). Review of the
> design, the algebra and the settlement rules is the purpose of this
> release. See [`DISCLAIMER.md`](DISCLAIMER.md) for provenance: the code
> and documentation were generated by AI systems under human direction.

## Architecture

| Component | Binary | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypha** (court) | `mycelium-server` | Admits signed acts, replays submitted work, rules on it, settles payments, keeps a hash-chained receipt ledger, folds the ledger into eras, and exchanges digests and co-signatures with peer hyphae. |
| **Mind** (client) | `mycelium-client` | Perceives tasks, learns the network's language (its *genome*), extracts rules, converses with other minds, and submits solves, words and sentences to a court. A mind can also hold a *seat* in a house run by another kernel. |
| **Soak harness** | `mycelium-soak` | Long-running load and durability runs against a mesh. |
| **Law forms** | `forms/` (crate) | The exact law forms shared with the other kernels that join the mesh, depending only on exact rationals. |

**Wire.** Nodes speak **Anastome** (`src/anastome/`), a closed set of
signed speech acts whose payloads are exact geometric objects: canonical
elements, coboundary chains and four-lane μ vectors. There are about forty
act kinds, among them percept, task, solve, speech, docket, settle, offer,
fold, fetch, syllabus, seat, handoff and challenge. Natural-language text is
never part of a payload.

**Language.** The language has three layers:
- **Letters:** a fixed alphabet of seventeen Cayley letters
  (`docs/ALPHABET.md`).
- **Words:** products of letters. A word is a vertex whose meaning is its
  versor.
- **Sentences:** forms built from words: vertices, faces and rings, glued
  along shared overlaps.

A rule a mind states about a task is a sentence. That sentence may carry
geometric **rule elements** (`src/transducer/sentence/form/rule_element.rs`):
- a *carriage*: a versor of Cl(4,1)(ℚ) applied once or iterated to a
  contact, with a gauge (hue) map;
- 2-chains bounded by a selection;
- 1-chains joining bodies;
- frame operations.

Elements that have an English name are glossed as clauses, such as *slide*,
*fill* or *echo*. The clause is a translation of the element; the element
is what goes on the wire.

**Clock.** Fleet time is **TUniversal**, a point `{origin, h, S:G:T:H, K}`
that is final once a majority of keepers hold it. No wall clock enters
consensus (`src/chronometry/`).

**Ledger.** Each hypha keeps its acts on a receipt chain. The chain folds
into eras, and witness hyphae co-sign each era's fold
(`docs/LEDGER_EPOCH_DESIGN.md`).

## POW++: the proofs of work

POW++ is a *family* of proofs. Each member is a distinct act with its own
settlement row on the ledger, and the members are never summed across
(`docs/MU-ECONOMY.md` §0; `src/economy/treasury.rs`, `enum Arm`). Every
payment row names the arm it pays. Rows that prove no work carry no arm:
genesis, stake, escrow, revocation, slash, burn, fee and subsidy.

| Arm | Name | The act that is proven | What settlement does | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **POUW** | Proof of useful work | A listed demand is **closed**. The court replays the submitted word or sentence on the task's family and measures the deflation it achieves: what it closes, lane by lane. | **Mints** the measured deflation and pays it to the closer. | `economy/escrow.rs`, `economy/settle.rs`, `server/cognition/court/install.rs` |
| **POWC** | Proof of work convergence | A demand one mind could not discharge is **closed at another perspective**: another mind's work, citing the first mind's receipt, discharges it. A word discovered by one mind and used by a later demand is the same relation. | **Mints** the citation's measured discharge to the closer (`settle_convergence`), and pays the cited discoverer by **transfer** from the user of the word (`settle_use`). | `economy/settle.rs` |
| **POWR** | Proof of work retirement | A word or form is **re-expressed over fewer letters**, by retirement along a geodesic or by citation; a refinement bid is replayed with the retired word excluded. | **Burns** the freed description and pays the retirer the compression yield. | `economy/settle.rs` (`settle_rewrite`), `server/cognition/book/rewrite.rs` |

### How POUW is divided on install

When a court installs a sentence on a second vantage (see
[Adjudication](#adjudication)), the POUW mint is divided according to the
local discharge each contribution achieves on the sentence's own task
family (`docs/MU-CLIENT.md` §5.4 and §5.6). It goes in this order:

1. **Cited forms:** each closed form the sentence glues in takes its own
   local discharge on the citing sentence's family.
2. **Taught words (D2′):** words the sentence's closed rings teach. A word
   already held pays its discoverer a use share. A new word is installed on
   its own act, and its speaker takes the base share.
3. **Composition premium / gluing (S):** what the sentence closes beyond
   the sections of its words goes to the sentence's voices.

Two further entries are recorded alongside these shares:

- **Attestations (E3):** a mind that verifiably agreed with a word becomes
  a party to its claim.
- **Convergence:** two minds whose words are different vertices but have
  the same section on one scene are each sealed as an attestation on the
  other's word.

A demand closed by another mind is paid as POWC (E2).

### Refusals are refinements

A court never refuses a submission silently. Every refusal names its cause
(for example `REFUSE_EDGE`, `REFUSE_RULE_TIE` or `REFUSE_MV_INEXACT`), and a
refusal is treated as information that refines the asker's model, not as
lost work. The obstruction calculus (OB1–OB5) is specified in
`docs/MU-RECURSION.md`.

## The unit of value: μ

μ is the currency, and it is **not a scalar**. It lives on four lanes:

| Lane | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **S** (spatial) | boosts and dilations |
| **G** (gauge) | hue cycles and moves |
| **T** (temporal) | null flux |
| **H** (holonomic) | turns |

A payment moves value only on the lanes the verified work moved. Lanes
never convert, and a comparison of two μ vectors returns a per-lane reading,
never a verdict (`docs/MU-ECONOMY.md` §1). A comment in the code headed
"nothing is squared" refers to this rule. It is enforced throughout: no
norm or sum collapses μ, a geometric quantity, or a reading to a single
number.

## Adjudication

- **Second vantage (47.3):** a word or sentence is installed only when two
  independent hyphae re-derive the *same vertex*. Equality is algebraic
  (`same_vertex`, projective equality of versors), never byte equality of
  encodings. Disagreeing re-derivations are refused as inconsistent data,
  never settled by a vote (`src/court/convergence.rs`).
- **Verdicts by replay:** solves are judged by replaying the submitted rule
  on pairs the submitter never saw (`src/court/verdict/`). A rule that
  states geometric elements is spoken on each scene and must reproduce the
  expected output exactly.
- **Keys, not tiers:** the court authenticates signing keys (Ed25519) and
  pays accounts. Any access policy belongs to the kernel that runs a house
  (`docs/CONCORDANCE.md`).

## The workload

The useful work is drawn from the **ARC-AGI-2** corpus: grid-transformation
tasks with training pairs and withheld test pairs. A mind's solve is useful
work only if the court's replay of the mind's rule reproduces the withheld
outputs. The corpus is not vendored; see
[Building and testing](#building-and-testing).

## Building and testing

Requirements: Rust 1.82 or later (developed on 1.95), and a Linux host.

```sh
cargo build --release            # mycelium-server, mycelium-client, mycelium-soak
cargo test --lib <module path>   # scoped suites, e.g. transducer::family::tests::elements
(cd xtask && cargo run)          # the gate: reading bound, then clippy -D warnings
```

The full library suite is long-running (some tests replay corpus tasks).
Run suites by module path.

**Corpus.** Tests that read ARC-AGI-2 expect the dataset at
`data/arc-agi-2/{training,evaluation}/*.json`, relative to the repository
root. The default can be overridden with `MYCELIUM_CORPUS`. Obtain the
dataset from the ARC Prize Foundation's public ARC-AGI-2 repository.

**Lints.** The crate denies `unsafe_code`, float arithmetic, lossy casts,
`unwrap`/`expect`/`panic` outside tests, and a set of disallowed types and
methods (`clippy.toml`). The coding standards are in
[`docs/MU-STANDARDS.md`](docs/MU-STANDARDS.md).

## Running a node

A node is a **hypha**: one `mycelium-server` process with its own home
directory, holding the court, the ledger and a task registry. A network is
a set of hyphae that share one **genesis**. The genesis is the chain hash
of the network's first receipt, written once by one hypha and adopted by
every other.

### What a home holds

| File | Written by | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `hypha.key` | `--init` | The node's Ed25519 seed (32 bytes). Its verifying key is the node's identity. Keep it private. |
| `bind`, `peers`, `genesis` | the operator, before the first boot | Provisioning, read once on the first boot (see below). |
| `mesh` | the node | The signed mesh record: bind, peers, genesis allocation, adopted origin, epoch. Built from the provisioning files on the first boot, which then removes those files. |
| `corpora/*.manifest` | `--sign-corpus` | A signed task corpus this court registers. |
| `ledger`, `line`, `eras`, `keeper/` | the node | The court's state: language, receipt chain, ledger eras, TUniversal keeper record. |

### Bringing up a new network

The steps below were run end to end on 2026-10-30. Three hyphae and one
client were on one host, and the SDK client solved and was paid for tasks
on the resulting network.

**1. Create an identity for every node.**

```sh
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/a --init     # prints:  genesis line: hypha <vk hex>
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/b --init
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/c --init
mycelium-client --home /srv/myc/v1 --init    # prints:  genesis line: voice <vk hex> <S> <G> <T> <H>
```

**2. Write the genesis allocation.** Collect every printed line into one
`genesis` file:
- one `hypha <vk>` line for each court of the network;
- one `voice <vk> S G T H` line for each client funded at genesis. The four
  numbers are its μ allocation on the S, G, T and H lanes.

Copy the identical file into every hypha's home. The hyphae named here are
bonded by every court, so each counts as an independent witness
([Adjudication](#adjudication)).

```text
hypha 44b00dee3eab19b73ca5f56bcc7ed4c45070fc2ae1dc2f4c319b0491888074d1
hypha be643e831919d4eb8a9053d9eaa34aa2c77f85790f5b23135ff344fe93a0e75e
hypha 5d10d3876c47bb51da7e2b6db7fda03f20f7b28835145dbe3810461a2fb0c7ff
voice 1eac4511991e6918a46c8ab7494149a1178079fa62d4588ade049922948eb7bb 8 8 1 8
```

**3. Write each hypha's address and peers.** Each hypha's home needs:
- `bind`: one line giving the address the hypha serves on. Use an address
  its peers can reach. A home with no `bind` file binds an ephemeral
  loopback port.
- `peers`: the other hyphae's addresses, one per line.

```sh
echo 127.0.0.1:7610 > /srv/myc/a/bind
printf '127.0.0.1:7611\n127.0.0.1:7612\n' > /srv/myc/a/peers
# likewise for b and c
```

**4. Register a task corpus.** One hypha named in the genesis signs the
corpus, and the manifest is copied to every hypha. Each court registers a
corpus only if a genesis hypha's key signed its manifest. The manifest
names the corpus directory by absolute path, and that path must exist on
every host.

```sh
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/a --sign-corpus /srv/corpus/arc-agi-2/training
cp /srv/myc/a/corpora/training.manifest /srv/myc/b/corpora/
cp /srv/myc/a/corpora/training.manifest /srv/myc/c/corpora/
```

**5. Start the network.** Exactly one hypha mints the genesis, once. Every
other hypha adopts it from a peer.

```sh
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/a --mint-genesis   # logs: court genesis <hash>… epoch 0 written
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/b
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/c
```

`--mint-genesis` is used only for this first start. Every later start,
including a's, omits it.

**6. Verify.**

```sh
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/b --check   # mesh: …, peers 2, genesis ef0a…, epoch 0 — and "this home will boot"
mycelium-server --home /srv/myc/a --console --reading court
```

`--check` reads a home without starting it: the key, the mesh record and
its adopted genesis, the ledger, and whether the home will boot on this
build. It exits 0 if the home will boot and 3 if it will not.

### Joining an existing network

A hypha joins an existing network by being named in the network's genesis
allocation. Run steps 1–3 with the network's `genesis` file and its
existing hyphae as `peers`, copy the corpus manifests (step 4), and start
**without** `--mint-genesis`. The node adopts the network's genesis from
its peers. Every hypha's `genesis` file must name the new node's key
before those hyphae's first boot, so membership is fixed when the network
is founded. See the status table below for nodes added later.

### Operating a node

- **Readings.** `mycelium-server --home DIR --console --reading <name>`
  prints a typed reading: `acts`, `court`, `economy`, `judged`,
  `dispatched`, `clock`, `memory`, `replication`, `agreements` and others.
  `--at HOST:PORT` reads another hypha.
- **Restarts** persist the ledger on SIGINT, SIGTERM and SIGHUP. Run
  `--check` before starting a new build on an existing home.
- **Ledger layout changes** are made as a *recut*: `scripts/recut` re-founds
  the network on a fresh genesis and **deletes court state**. A build that
  cannot read a home's ledger refuses to boot rather than overwrite it.
- **systemd.** `ops/mycelium-{server,client}@.service` are user-unit
  templates; set the checkout path in them.

### Status of node operation

| Capability | Status |
|---|---|
| Node identity (`--init`), provisioning, signed mesh record | **Finished**, verified end to end |
| Genesis minted by one hypha and adopted by its peers | **Finished**, verified |
| Peer exchange: digests, receipts, ledger eras, co-signed folds | **Finished**, verified on one host |
| Corpus signing (`--sign-corpus`) and registration | **Finished**, verified |
| Task dispatch to clients, solve adjudication by replay, payment | **Finished**, verified with the SDK client |
| `--check` preflight, typed console readings | **Finished** |
| **Adding a hypha after genesis.** It connects, exchanges digests and has its receipts admitted, but no court holds a bond for it, so it is never an independent witness and is not funded. | **Under review**: post-genesis bonding is not specified. |
| **Multi-host / wide-area deployment.** Acts are Ed25519-signed and verified on admission, but the transport is plain TCP without encryption, and deployment has been exercised only on one host. | **Under review** |
| **Changing a node's bind or peers after its first boot.** The provisioning files are read once into the signed record, and no command edits the record. | **Under review** |
| Ledger layout migration without a recut | **Under review**: a layout change requires a recut. |

## Building a client with the SDK

A client is any program that holds a voice key and speaks Anastome acts to
a hypha. The SDK is the `mycelium` library crate's `sdk` module, together
with the wire codecs in `anastome::dialogue` and the rule machinery in
`transducer`. The reference mind (`mycelium-client`) is one client built
on it. [`examples/sdk_client.rs`](examples/sdk_client.rs) is a complete
minimal client in about 150 lines.

### Dependency

```toml
[dependencies]
mycelium = { git = "https://github.com/SAIOS-SAMN/mycelium" }   # or path = "../mycelium"
ed25519-dalek = "2.1"
```

### The client loop

```rust
use mycelium::anastome::dialogue;
use mycelium::court::identity::HyphaKey;
use mycelium::sdk::{Kernel, Session};
use mycelium::transducer::family::{extract_rule, mint_sentence_why, speak::speak_rule, NoCrystal};

// 1. Identity: a voice key, minted once and kept (32-byte Ed25519 seed).
let key = HyphaKey::load_or_mint("voice.key");
// 2. Session: one hypha's address.
let session = Session::open("127.0.0.1:7610", Kernel::from_key(key.sk.clone()));
// 3. Work: ask the court for a task. The envelope carries train pairs and
//    test inputs, never the test outputs. An empty reply means no work.
let reply = session.speak(dialogue::encode_task_request(&key.vk_bytes()))?;
let task = dialogue::decode_task_envelope(&reply).ok_or("no task")?;
// 4. Rule: extract a rule from the train pairs, mint it as an element the
//    court can replay, and speak it on each test input.
let mut budget = mycelium::physics::Physics::default().stroke_n(1 << 20);
let rule = extract_rule(&task.train, &mut budget).ok_or("no rule")?;
let element = mint_sentence_why(&rule, &task.train, &NoCrystal, 0)?;
let outputs: Vec<_> = task.tests.iter()
    .map(|x| speak_rule(&rule, x, &NoCrystal).and_then(|s| s.grid()))
    .collect::<Option<_>>().ok_or("rule does not speak a test")?;
// 5. Solve: outputs and the rule's tape, signed by the voice the court pays.
let solve = dialogue::encode_solve_with_rule(&key.sk, &task.id, &outputs,
                                             Some(&element.to_bytes().ok_or("tape")?)).ok_or("solve")?;
let verdict = session.speak(solve)?;
let (code, paid_mu, refusal, _modes) = dialogue::decode_solve_verdict(&verdict).ok_or("verdict")?;
```

The verdict codes are:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SOLVE_BOUNTY` | First verified closure of the task. The μ shown is paid. |
| `SOLVE_FLOOR` | A verified re-solve of a task already closed. |
| `SOLVE_NOTHING` | Final: the replay refused the rule. `refusal` names why, e.g. "form leaves a remainder on the family". |
| `SOLVE_UNREAD` | The court's stroke ran out before the replay ruled. Send the same solve again and the replay resumes. |

Running the example against a hypha:

```sh
cargo build --release --example sdk_client
target/release/examples/sdk_client /srv/myc/v1 127.0.0.1:7610 8
#   voice 1eac4511…
#   task 65b0c3689cfe3a37… solved: first verified closure (bounty) — paid μ [0, 24, 0, 0]
#   task da0dfd765fbe3521… not solved (final) — refused: form leaves a remainder on the family
#   task 12897f8fa760a993… not sent: the rule does not mint over its family
```

### Stating your own rules

A client is not limited to the bundled extractor. There are two other ways
to state a rule:
- **Typed steps.** `sdk::rule::sentence(&[Step], family, crystal)` mints a
  rule from steps (select, create, crop, D4 symmetries, hue moves) and
  refuses by name any rule that does not close its family.
- **Rule elements.** A rule can be stated directly as geometric elements
  (`transducer::sentence::form::rule_element`: carriages, chains, joins,
  frames) on a `Form`.

Either way, the court replays the rule on withheld pairs; the client's
claim is never taken on trust.

## Known issues

- **Unbounded ask queue on the client (open).** On the 2026-10-27 build
  (commit `a6336680`), the queue of court requests a mind has posted but
  not yet taken an answer for grows without bound after a joint restart of
  hyphae and minds: up to about 6×10⁵ entries within an hour, against a
  steady state of 0–7 before. Court tasks stop reaching the minds. A
  per-path reading of the queue was added (`d905bf42`) to localize the
  posting path; the cause is not yet identified.
- **Sentences rarely close rings.** Most sentences the extractor writes
  are trees, so the gluing share (S) seldom pays.
- **Coverage of the task corpus is narrow.** The act reader states a rule
  for roughly 9% of the ARC-AGI-2 training tasks, and leave-one-out
  re-derivation holds on about 9% of held-out pairs.

## Where review is most useful

1. **Exactness.** Whether any path admits an approximation, a silent
   default, or a projection of a multivector or μ vector to a scalar.
2. **Settlement soundness.** Whether POUW, POWC and POWR can be minted
   twice for one act, paid to oneself, or paid on lanes the work did not
   move (`src/economy/`, `src/server/cognition/court/install.rs`).
3. **Adjudication.** The second-vantage rule and `same_vertex` as the
   convergence test (`src/court/convergence.rs`); replay and refusal
   naming (`src/court/verdict/`).
4. **Wire canonicality.** One encoding per object (`put_canonical`,
   `rule_element::put_elements` / `take_elements`), and decoders that
   refuse non-canonical input.
5. **The client concurrency model.** Carriers on their own threads, answers
   taken in order (`src/client/sdk/entity/hypha.rs`), in light of the open
   queue regression.
