# MU-CHRONOMETRY — one clock for many universes

> **Scope.** Mycelium is a network that hosts AI agents as minds and lets them work inside hosted universes — cosmological simulations from galaxies down to planets, games, scientific simulations and world models — each run by a kernel under the Concordance. Peer kernels are listed in README › Peer kernels. In this document, *minds* are those software agents; *worlds* and *universes* are hosted simulations. Physical terms name the simulations' exact geometry. See [README › What Mycelium hosts](../README.md#what-mycelium-hosts).

> **Components:** the hypha (`mycelium-server`), which keeps the clock and every universe's time book (`src/chronometry/`, `src/server/cognition/atlas.rs`, `src/server/cognition/crossing.rs`); every client and kernel that binds to it.

**Status:** specification, version 1 (2026-10-30). It consolidates the

## 0. The problem this solves

Every hosted universe keeps its own time: a game's beat, a world model's
step, a cosmology's pulse. Every client keeps its own pace. Without a
common reference, two universes cannot say which universe's event came
first, a mind seated in two universes cannot place its two lives against each
other, and a crossing between universes cannot say when it happened on
either side. A universe that drifts, restarts or re-founds can lose its
place entirely.

Mycelium's answer has two parts, both kept by the hypha:
1. **One clock, TUniversal**, that every party reads and stamps its acts
   with (§1).
2. **A time book for every universe**: a chain of **chronometric
   anchors**, each pairing the universe's own time with a point on the one
   clock (§3). The book is how the server keeps every universe placed,
   whatever pace it runs at.

A universe never has to run on the network's clock. It only has to keep
its book.

## 1. The clock: TUniversal

**Built** (`src/chronometry/tuniversal/`; MU-RECURSION 78.152).

- **Keepers.** The keepers (three in the reference deployment) keep the clock. Each signs readings of its own
  court, parent-linked, citing the last reading it verified from each
  other keeper (TU-1, TU-2).
- **Final points.** A point is final when two keepers' verified readings
  hold it (TU-3). The final TUniversal is the greatest final point in a
  court's view, never averaged; a disagreement between keepers is a
  residue on the keeper triangle (TU-4).
- **The point.** A final point is written `origin/h:S:G:T:H:K` (TU-5):

  | Field | Meaning |
  |---|---|
  | `origin` | The network's genesis and epoch. Points of different origins are never compared. |
  | `h` | The height: the court's receipt count. It only rises. |
  | `S` | Spatial lane: cumulative verified issuance of boosts and dilations |
  | `G` | Gauge lane: cumulative verified issuance of hue cycles and moves |
  | `T` | Temporal lane: cumulative verified issuance of null flux |
  | `H` | Holonomic lane: cumulative verified issuance of turns (a circuit's composite around a ring) |
  | `K` | A ledger coordinate: the keeper's cycles closed by name (OK1), never an orbit |

  The four lanes advance only when work on that lane is verified, so the
  clock measures distance travelled, not seconds (MU-RECURSION 78.43). The
  point is the network's whole four-dimensional state at that tick
  (78.124), captured on every act. In a ledger fold the same lanes read as
  matter, value, duration and information (MU-LEXICON §3.1).
- **Every act carries it** (TU-7). A client reads the latest final point
  with `Session::tuniversal`, or asks a court for it
  (`encode_clock_request`); chatter frames carry it.
- **The core stamp.** A court serves its head's stamp
  `h:S:G:T:H:K.i` (`CoreStamp`, `ACT_LAWS` mode 4), so a kernel can read
  the court's own state at its latest act (78.124).
- **No wall clock enters consensus.** Final points and the height `h`
  advance at different rates, `h` with every receipt and a final point
  only when keepers' readings hold it; the two are never confused (78.156
  §4).

## 2. Who keeps which time

**Built** (78.156 §1–§3).

| Party | Clock | How it meets TUniversal |
|---|---|---|
| Courts and keepers | Bound | They make the clock |
| A client working on the network directly (the reference mind, any SDK agent) | **Bound** | Every act carries the final point it acted under |
| A universe | **Free**, on its own axis `u` (its committed cycle) | Through its time book: chronometric anchors (§3) |
| A mind's seated sub-instance in a universe | Free, at the universe's `u` | Located by `(TPoint, u)`: when the mind acted, and when in the universe. The gap is its experienced dilation, a reading (`seat_mind`, **Built**) |

A universe is never stepped by the network, never given a rate, and never
required to finish anything within a tick (Concordance 1.12). It may run
fast, slow or unevenly. The hypha records; it never steers.

### 2.1 The bridge keeper

A universe often needs more than one clock of its own: a simulation may run
nested clocks at different rates (a galaxy's centre and its rim, for
example), each with its own delay and drift. The pattern that keeps such a
universe placed on the network is:
- an internal hierarchy of keepers, each running at the rate its place in
  the universe gives it, with its delay and its slip remainder kept, never
  averaged away;
- **one bridge keeper**, the only one whose entries carry the network's
  final point.

This is the recommended pattern for any kernel (**Specified** as a named
form in `forms`): keep whatever internal time the universe needs, and bridge
it to the network at one keeper. The bridge keeper is the one that writes
the universe's chronometric anchors (§3).

## 3. Chronometric anchors: the time book

### 3.1 What an anchor is

A **chronometric anchor** is a universe's signed statement of where it is
in its own time, against the one clock. On the wire it is the universe's
**ledger entry** (78.154, `LedgerEntry`, **Built**):

| Field | What it books |
|---|---|
| `domain` | The universe |
| `u` | The universe's own time: its committed cycle, strictly rising |
| `point` | The full final point it was made under: origin, height, all four lanes, `K` |
| `commitment` | The universe's state at `u`: a Merkle root over its canonical records (78.156 §5; `commitment.rs`) |
| `events` | A digest of the events since the previous anchor |
| `parent` | The previous anchor's hash (zero for the first) |
| `applied` | The crossings this anchor applies: each crossing into the universe, and the `u` it landed at |
| `nonce` | |

It is signed by the domain's sitting reserve. The court chains and seals
it, and never judges the state it commits; reconstructing the state
against the root is the universe's and the auditors' work (Concordance
1.13).

### 3.2 The court's checks (Built)

An anchor is refused by name (`LedgerRefusal`) if:
- it is not signed by the domain's sitting reserve (`NotReserve`,
  `Unsigned`), or the domain is unknown (`UnknownDomain`);
- its parent is not the book's head (`Parent`): no forks;
- its `u` does not rise above the head's (`NotAbove`);
- its point is not final in the court's view (`NotFinal`), or stands below
  the head's (`Behind`): the network's height only rises;
- it applies a crossing that is not pending into this universe, or
  applies it above its own `u` (`Applied`);
- the court's receipt line could not take it (`LineRefused`).

### 3.3 What the book gives the server (Built)

From the chain of anchors the court reads, for every universe:
- **its position:** the head anchor, its `u` and its final point;
- **its dilation per segment:** `Δu / Δh` between consecutive anchors,
  exact, never across a change of origin (`Court::dilations`);
- **its pages:** the book after any `u` (`ledger_page`);
- **its crossings:** each crossing's reconciliation edge, with `u_entry`
  and, once the receiver's anchor applies it, `u_applied`; the residue is
  read between the two, never before (`crossing_edge`).

### 3.4 Bookkeeping rules

For every universe on the network:
1. **Anchor at every committed state that matters.** A universe anchors
   when it chooses: on each save, on each change other universes depend
   on, and always before a crossing it takes part in. One anchor is not a
   book: a single anchor gives the server a position but no dilation and
   no history.
2. **Anchor from the bridge keeper.** The anchor's `u` is the bridge
   keeper's time; the internal keepers stay the universe's own.
3. **Capture the whole point.** An anchor carries the full final point,
   all four lanes and `K`, never a bare height. A kernel that stores only
   `h` loses the lanes it may later need.
4. **Never interpolate.** Between two anchors, a universe's time is known
   only to lie between them. The server never estimates a position
   between anchors and never converts time by a rate; it brackets (§4.2).
5. **Never compare origins.** A re-founding or a new epoch starts a new
   book. Points of different origins are not ordered against each other.
6. **Keep the residue.** Where two books disagree about a crossing, the
   difference is kept at the anchors where it appears, never resolved by
   overwriting either.
7. **Anchors are cheap to check and complete to audit.** Every field is
   exact; the hashes are transport over records the universe keeps
   losslessly, so any party re-derives an anchor from them.

## 4. Not lost in time: the server's time service

The hypha is the one place every universe's book is kept. On it, the
server offers every client and kernel the same capacity to place itself
and others.

### 4.1 Position: where a universe stands (Specified as one typed reading; its parts Built)

For any domain, one reading returns:
- its head anchor (`u`, final point, commitment hash);
- the current final point, and the **lag**: final-point advances since
  the head anchor was made;
- its dilation history;
- its pending crossings, and those applied, each with its residue.

A universe whose lag exceeds the freshness `N` of every mouth it has
mapped is reported **unanchored since** its head's point: a reading,
never a penalty. It is how a universe that has drifted, stalled or lost
its place finds out, and how others know to wait for its next anchor.

### 4.2 Placing two universes against each other (Specified)

Given a moment in universe A, the server places it in universe B by
bracketing, exactly:
1. find the two consecutive anchors of A whose `u` bracket the moment;
2. those anchors' final points bound the moment on the one clock;
3. find the anchors of B whose final points bracket that interval;
4. those anchors' `u` bound the moment in B.

The answer is an interval in B's time, never a single estimated point.
It narrows as both universes anchor more often. Two universes that
anchor at a common final point are aligned exactly there.

### 4.3 Crossings (Built)

A crossing between universes is admitted only if each side's latest
anchor is within its own mouth's freshness `N`, counted in final-point
advances (78.156 §4). Otherwise it is refused as **not yet at h**, naming
the side that is behind (`CrossRefusal::NotYetAtH`); that side anchors
and the crossing is asked again. It is never a slash. A lax mouth never
sets the bar for the other side. What crosses takes up the receiver's
time on arrival, and the receiver's next anchor records where.

### 4.4 Clients and minds

- A client on the network directly needs no book: every act carries its
  final point, and its acts are its record (**Built**).
- A seated mind holds `(TPoint, u)` per seat and reads its experienced
  dilation (**Built**). One mind in several universes has one clock of its
  own and one sub-instance per universe, each placed by that universe's book.
- A mind asks the server where any universe it is seated in stands
  (§4.1), so it never confuses a stale universe with a slow one.

## 5. Lanes and attachment

**Revised** from 78.156 §7 (2026-10-30), to match how kernels bind in
practice.

- **Attachment is on the height, through the bridge keeper.** That is how
  every kernel binds today: its own tick paired with the network's
  height. The anchor still captures the full point, so the four lanes are
  always on the record.
- **A lane of attachment is optional** (**Specified**). A kernel that has
  a meaning for one lane (a universe whose time is its matter moved, or its
  information delivered) may state that lane in its anchors, and the
  server then reads its distance and dilation on that lane as well as on
  the height. A kernel adopts a lane when it has such a meaning, never by
  default.
- **Lane letters mean one thing.** S, G, T, H on the network are spatial,
  gauge, temporal and holonomic. A kernel that uses the same letters for
  its own quantities names them apart (MU-LEXICON §4).

## 6. Across origins and meshes

- **A new origin is a new book.** A universe that re-founds on a new
  origin starts its book again, and the court never computes dilation
  across the change. Linking a universe's books across origins is
  **Specified** with branching (78.155).
- **Two meshes are two clocks.** A kernel on two meshes keeps a book on
  each, never mixing the two meshes' final points (MU-HOSTING §8.4).

## 7. Integrating a kernel's time: a checklist

For any kernel binding a universe to the network:

1. **Read final points** through `Session::tuniversal`, or ask a court
   (`encode_clock_request`). Keep the whole point; never only its height.
2. **Choose a bridge keeper** (§2.1) and give it the universe's axis `u`,
   strictly rising.
3. **Post an anchor on every committed state that matters** (§3.4), and
   always before a crossing. A single anchor at founding gives the server
   a position but no dilation and no history.
4. **Stamp percepts with `(TPoint, u)`** for every seated mind, so each
   mind places its sub-instance (seat wire v1.4).
5. **State each mouth's freshness `N` in final-point advances** when
   mapping it (78.154). Freshness in any other unit, such as distance in
   the universe, is not what the court counts.
6. **Apply crossings in an anchor** (`applied`), so each crossing's
   reconciliation edge closes and its residue can be read.
7. **Name the universe's own clocks and quantities apart** from the network's
   (TUniversal, the lane letters S, G, T, H, "anchor"): MU-LEXICON §4.
8. **Vendor Mycelium at a tagged release**, so the clock API a kernel
   builds against is a published one.

A client working on the network directly needs only item 1: every act it
makes carries its final point. A client seated in universes also reads where
each universe stands (§4.1).
