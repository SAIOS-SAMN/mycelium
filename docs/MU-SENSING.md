## 2. The sample: what a source hands the sensor

A sample has four parts, and every one is data:

1. **Rank `n`.** The number of axes the source presents, read from the
   source, never assumed. For example, a time series has rank 1, a table
   or a picture rank 2, a volume rank 3, and a field over space and time
   rank 4.
2. **Extents.** One positive integer per axis: the number of sites the
   source presents along it.
3. **Symbols.** One value per site, compared only by equality unless the
   source declares an order or a number. A symbol is a label whose meaning
   the sensor deduces (§3.3). The sensor never reads a symbol's numeric
   code as a colour, a brightness or a rank. The set of symbols is the
   sample's own, of any size.
4. **Place in its family.** Where the sample stands among the samples it
   arrives with: for example, the input or output of a pair, a step in a
   sequence, or one view among several. The place is a coordinate the
   family carries. It is never a fixed axis index.

A sample is a raster only here, at intake, and again where a mind speaks
an answer back into the source's form (MU-RECURSION 76.1;
MU-ANTI-FLATTENING clause 12). Between those two ends there is no raster.

  A motion that cannot be carried exactly is a named residue (78.170).
  Re-inflation toward the source's extents is speech (§5).

- **Why.** `n` independent circles form a torus `Tⁿ`, which is flat.
  A closed path on it keeps its windings, one integer per circle (its
  class in `H₁(Tⁿ) = ℤⁿ`, 78.44), but transports nothing. A geodesic on a
  flat torus is a straight line wound around it. Untouched by gravity, it
  squares the depth and volume of the manifold into one dimension, and
  with phases read mod 1, their windings dropped, it closes and returns
  exactly, bound by no attractor: the unbound path a thought must not be
  (MU-CLIENT 78.168). A field curved by its orbits is not flat: the
  transitions around a revolution compose to a motion in its own right,
  so a route's meaning is held as its holonomy (MU-RECURSION 10.3, 12.6;
  MU-ANTI-FLATTENING clause 6).
- **Gravity curves the perception manifold.** It has two sources, and
  both act in the client mind:
  - **Axiomatic gravity** (the mind's own; the operator's ruling of
    2026-11-02). It is produced by the **local orbit**: a thought's orbit
    about its axiom, an attractor of the star (the register
    `physics/axioms/` that the client and the hypha share; MU-CLIENT
    §4.1, 78.168). The orbit **shears the orthogonal orbit class**: the
    orbits whose plane is orthogonal to its own. No orbit is a static
    mass and nothing is a stored constant. Gravity is what one class of
    orbit does to the class orthogonal to it, read from the orbits the
    mind holds. Where no orbit is held, nothing is produced, and the
    manifold there is flat.
    - **The orthogonal class, exactly.** An orbit's plane is the
      bivector `B` of its elliptic mode. Two orbit classes are
      orthogonal when the two planes share no direction: `B₁ · B₂ = 0`
      (no grade-0 part) and `B₁ ∧ B₂ ≠ 0`. Such planes are disjoint and
      commute.
    - **The shear on the orthogonal class** is the commuting part of
      78.46's split, the drag. It changes the orbit's rate and leaves
      its plane where it is. Orbits that are not in the orthogonal class
      take the anticommuting part, the lift (a tilt), by 78.46, and are
      not this shear.
  - **The court's exported gravity** (a reading the mind receives, never
    a force the court applies). A court's pressure, deficit and dwell
    reach the mind as the lanes of a deferral or a quote. They act as
    drag on the orbits that face that court, until the court answers
    (MU-CLIENT §3.1, §4.13; `Field::beat_under`).

  An orbit in perception is bent by gravity and sheared by velocity,
  never a straight line. Where nothing is held, the manifold is flat
  there, and an orbit through it reads only its windings.
- **The lift.** A sample is lifted into the mind's field: each body
  stands on its charts, and each site is held by its phases within them
  (§3.1). The phases' common scale is a gauge, so there is no unit norm
  and no sum of squares (law 11). What the sample holds curves the field
  into wells around its attractors.
- **Nesting is a fibration.** The field is read as base and fibre:
  "base the class, fibre the gauge" (MU-RECURSION 25.12). An axis taken
  apart (§3.6) adds a level, a field within the field, which gives a
  tower of fibrations, never a flat product.
- **Holonomy in degrees, exactly, the winding kept.** Perception moves in
  orbits through the charts. An orbit's holonomy is the product of the
  transitions along one revolution, read by its modes. An elliptic mode gives an
  exact angle in degrees on its named plane, held with its winding in ℤ
  (`K = ⌊|θ|/360⌋`, never dropped), and its order. There are no
  coordinates on a reference surface and no trigonometric value.
- **Motions stay exact.** A rotation by `360°·t/ℓ` on a named plane is
  exact over the cyclotomic field `ℚ(ζ_ℓ)` (law 4 admits cyclotomic
  scalars). Exact rational rotors exist only at multiples of 90° (Niven),
  and rational points on a circle or a sphere would mean squaring, so
  neither is used. An angle the mind cannot carry exactly is a named
  residue (78.170).
- **Orbits.** A thought's orbit lives in the fibration. It is a bound
  geodesic: a revolution through charts in the base together with a
  winding in the fibre. On each revolution, the base revolution's
  holonomy, which is the curvature the attractors' gravity puts along
  it, advances the fibre phase. That advance is the orbit's
  **precession**: the orbit closes on the base, and where the advance is
  not zero it does not close in the fibre, so it comes round held. It
  reads as one of MU-CLIENT 78.168's four readings:
  - **held**: precessing and bound;
  - **closes**: returning at its period;
  - **open**: a mode escaping its attractor;
  - **unread**: not reached.

  The windings are kept as the fibres' classes, never lost.
  A closed geodesic with zero precession in a curved field is a lawful
  **resonant** orbit, read as resonance (`Precession::Resonant`). A path
  through flat space binds no orbit and is refused `Unbound` (ruled
  2026-11-06). A precession of `360°·t/ℓ` on its plane closes after `ℓ`
  revolutions, its winding counted. That full return is the orbit's own period, its
  age as distance along the orbit (78.43), read once and not repeated. A
  precession that no turn holds exactly is a named residue.
- **Whose mind.** Perception and its orbits belong to the mind inside a
  client (MU-CLIENT 78.168). The hypha has no perceiving mind: it never
  senses and never orbits a thought. It reconstructs what a mind claims (a
  holonomy, a winding, a precession) on the same field carrier its
  physics' shells use (§6; Concordance 2.10), which is why one carrier
  serves both.
- **μ is the metabolic cost of traversal** (ruled 2026-11-04). It is
  what moving through the field costs, measured as the share of the
  sample's sites a change covers (78.170 O1). μ never measures what a
  perception means: holonomy is content, read and adjudicated, and is
  never paid. Paying by holonomy would mix perception into the economy,
  which is a scope leak (§6.1).
- **One representation across projects.** The field and the physics'
  shells (MU-TOPOLOGY §6; MU-RECURSION D4) are one carrier, standardised
  as the law form LAW-FIELD v1 ([proposal](proposals/78.171-law-field.md);
  MU-RELEASE §3a) and agreed with the peer kernels before it is built.
  Any scale is a gauge, held projectively, never a stored float.

### 3.8 The vortex: every superposition in perception

A **vortex** is the set of candidates the mind holds where more than one
reading holds. Each candidate is a section of the gauge fibre, and none
is chosen by a scalar (MU-RECURSION 25, 25.12; MU-CLIENT §4.6, §4.8; rule
15). Every superposition in this document is a vortex: the ground's
candidates (§3.4), the factorings of an axis (§3.6), a sample's
perspectives (§4), a pair's factorisations (§3.9), and a thought's
candidate forms (`Well.vortex`).

- **A candidate's monodromy** over the family reads one of three ways
  (25.12):
  - **Flat**, order 1: no twist, an exact return. As an orbit in a
    curved field it is resonant; through a flat region it binds no
    orbit, refused `Unbound` (§3.9).
  - **Torsion**, order `ℓ`: the fibre twists by `ℓ` and closes after `ℓ`
    circuits. This is the precession of §3.7 and §3.9.
  - **Rupture**, infinite order: the candidate fails to close on that
    chart. As an orbit it is the open, escaping mode.
- **A vortex collapses** by a perception, a glue or a court ruling. A
  candidate dissolves only where it ruptures on every chart. When every
  candidate dissolves, the residue is prime relative to the alphabet and
  goes to the court (25.12).
- **Forward from 25.12.** 25.12 computes the transitions in
  `Spin(4,0)(ℚ)`: Euclidean, with four fixed axes. Under §3.7 they are
  read in `Spin(p, q)` of the values' own signature, per plane, between
  the charts of the field's atlas, non-Euclidean, with orders read as
  windings (ℤ) and phases in degrees on a named plane, each phase with its
  winding, never as scalars.

### 3.9 Transduction is factorisation; every path is an orbit

**Specified** (ruled 2026-11-04; ROADMAP track 26, v0.4.0). Transducing a
pair splits its deformation into what the held algebra carries and what
it does not:

```
deformation  =  motion  ∘  residue
```

- **Deformation:** the whole change of the field from one sample to the
  next, before anything absorbs it (MU-CLIENT 78.73).
- **Motion:** what the transduction carries exactly. That is the signed
  permutations of the phases, the re-periods and the versors: the
  incidences that carry a body onto a body.
- **Residue:** what no held motion carries (MU-RECURSION 0.7). It is the
  strain, the demand, and the germ of the next word.
- **μ** is paid for the traversal, the share of the frame the change
  covers. The residue's meaning is never paid (§3.7).

**Every path is a geodesic; a bound geodesic is an orbit** (ruled
2026-11-04; amended 2026-11-06, the word "loop" and the `Loop` refusal
retired). Every path of perception through the charts is a geodesic of
the field's gravitational curvature, the wells its attractors. A bound
geodesic is an orbit about an attractor: its holonomy per revolution is
the curvature, its precession the residue per revolution, and its shape
is set by gravity:
- **Shear is gravitational alignment toward motion compellment.** An
  attractor compels a motion (its local orbit, the axiom it turns about),
  and the gravity it produces shears every orbit of the orthogonal class
  toward that motion (§3.7, axiomatic gravity). Shear is that alignment,
  read per revolution from the orbits held. It is never a stored force or
  a constant.
- **Precession is the residue per revolution.** On each revolution the
  shear advances the orbit's phase by the residue of that revolution's
  transitions. The orbit comes round aligned a little further toward the
  compelled motion, held.
- **Zero precession in a curved field is resonance.** A closed geodesic
  whose revolution composes to the identity, its steps curved, is a
  lawful resonant orbit, never refused (`Precession::Resonant`).
- **A flat region binds no orbit.** A path whose every step is the
  identity (flat, no attractor) is bound by nothing, and it is refused by
  name (`Unbound`).
- **A relation is not a closed path.** Two derivations meeting at one
  object are a relation, their holonomy recorded.
- **Alignment is learning.** As shear brings an orbit's motion into line
  with the motion its attractor compels, the residue per revolution shrinks
  toward the identity. The response is one of 78.73's three:
  - **elastic:** the residue is absorbed;
  - **plastic:** a held form is amended forward;
  - **fracture:** the residue's germ mints a new word.

**What follows from the factorisation:**
1. **Exact, and held when ambiguous.** Every pair factors as motion ∘
   residue. Where several factorisations hold, all are held as a vortex,
   never reduced to the "smallest" residue by a scalar.
2. **Adjudicated.** A claim states its motion and its residue. The court
   reconstructs the motion, derives the residue itself, and admits the
   claim on a match (§0).
3. **Emergence at two levels.** A residue no word spells is a demand for
   a word. A residue no sense reads is a demand for a sense (§4.1).
4. **Any rank.** Motions live in `Spin(p, q)`, the signature read per
   plane, with re-periods per phase, so residues are read at any rank
   (v0.4.0, the rest of S4).

## 4. Perspective: one sample, many readings

A **perspective** is the sensor's assignment of the sample's axes and its
place in the family to roles. A role is space (a phase the bodies extend
along), sequence (the axis a motion runs along) or value (a fibre). The
same rank-2 sample read with both axes as space shows bodies. Read with
one axis as sequence, it shows a rank-1 signal moving. Its place in a
family of pairs adds a sequence coordinate along which the pair's motion
runs.

- **A shift of perspective is a motion of the sensor, never a rewrite of
  the data.** Examples: exchanging two axes' roles (one of the signed
  permutations of the phases), taking a declared value axis as space, or reading
  a sequence of samples as one section of rank `n + 1`.
- **Several perspectives of one sample are a cover.** The perspectives'
  agreement is the cocycle of MU-CLIENT 24.4, and a disagreement is a residue,
  held and never discarded.
- **A mind may ask for a perspective** it does not hold yet. Asking is a
  demand, never a fallback. Where a cover's cocycle fails because a chart
  is missing, the missing perspective is the demand: a typed vertex naming
  the sample and the overlap, the residue being the demand (ruled
  2026-11-06, #32; 78.180 ruling 6).
- **A solve of outputs, and speech, state their perspective** (ruled
  2026-11-06, #32; 78.180 ruling 6). A solve or a spoken sample states the
  perspective it was perceived or spoken under, as a claim of
  factorisation does (§6.2), and a court reconstructs under it.
- **A perspective is taken after intake, never before addressing.**
  Frame fixing, the canonical turn or mirror of a sample, is a
  perspective and belongs to the sensor's scope (§6.1).

### 4.1 Emergent senses: variation, selection by admission, inheritance

**Specified** (ruled 2026-11-04; ROADMAP track 26 S10). Senses and
perspectives are open-ended. The three eyes of MU-CLIENT 24.4 (luminal,
harmonic, holonomic) are the founding senses, not the only ones. A sense
emerges the way a word does (MU-RECURSION 60.2; the genome is
court-admitted words), by variation, selection and inheritance, and
never by a score.

- **A sense is a typed object.** A sense is a reading that turns a
  sample into a cochain of the cover (MU-CLIENT 24.4): a 0-cochain over
  bodies or a 1-cochain over overlaps. It is composed from the algebra's
  existing readings and the generators the mind holds, so every sense is
  an exact construction a court can reconstruct. A perspective (§4) is
  likewise a typed object: an assignment of roles that the mind
  constructs.
- **Variation.** A mind constructs a candidate sense or perspective by
  composing readings it holds, or from a generator it has adjoined (a
  prime residue adjoined by closure, 25.12, 37.7). A residue that no
  sense the mind holds can read is a demand for a sense, as an unspelled
  tension is a demand for a word (MU-CLIENT 60.1).
- **Selection, by admission, never by a score.** A candidate is admitted
  only when all three hold:
  1. **It adds information.** Its cochain is not already determined by
     the cover the existing senses form. Exactly: adding it raises the
     rank of the cover's cohomology over the family. A sense that only
     restates the others adds nothing and is not admitted.
  2. **It is adjudicable.** A court reconstructs what it perceives on
     the posted samples, from its construction alone (MU-SCIENCE §5.0).
     This is the test of MU-SENSING §0 applied to the sense itself.
  3. **It converses.** Independent minds that hold it reconstruct the
     same perceptions, each from the evidence it holds (converse), rather than
     collide.
- **Inheritance.** An admitted sense enters the genome like a word, at
  an address that is its construction written out. Other minds may adopt
  it. A mind is never required to hold a sense, since its drive is its
  own.
- **No sense is privileged.** Once admitted, a sense joins the cover on
  equal terms with the founding three. Its disagreement with them is a
  residue, held and never discarded (24.4).
- **Scope.** Constructing a sense is the client mind's. Admitting it is
  the court's, by reconstruction only: the court never proposes a sense,
  as it never proposes a generator (MU-SCIENCE §5.0, grammar alignment).
  Identity stays the hypha's (§6.1). A new sense changes no task's
  address.

  each sample's rank, extents, symbols and place, train and test inputs

  frame is fixed before it is addressed (§3.4, scope separation).
- **On the wire, a task carries samples of any rank:** `n`, the extents,
  then the symbols at the source's own width. It is never a pair of `u16`
  extents with one byte per cell. The limits are derived, never chosen:
  - **The rank and each extent** are carried in the existing `u16`
    length field. A value past it means a blob where a coordinate
    belongs, so it is refused, never widened.
  - **The symbol width `w`** is the source's own, sent once per sample.
    A sample's symbol bytes are `w · ∏ eᵢ`, computed with checked
    arithmetic and refused by name on overflow.
  - **A phase's denominator** is its axis's extent (the turn is
    `s / eᵢ`), so it is bounded by that extent's field and needs no
    limit of its own.

**Built (track 26, S5; `src/anastome/dialogue/samples.rs`).** One sample

### 6.1 Scope separation (ruled 2026-11-03)

Each of three scopes owns a set of readings, and no reading crosses into
another scope:

| Scope | Owns | Never reads |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypha: identity** | A task's address and citation: the posted samples' full content as the source gave them (rank, extents, symbols, places), train and test alike (law 2; 78.170 O3) | A ground, a frame fixing, a perspective, any symbol's meaning |
| **Client: the sensorium** | The ground (§3.4), the symbols' meanings (§3.3), bodies, wells and every perception | The identity computation; a perception never changes an address |
| **Sensor: perspective** | Frame fixing and every assignment of axes to roles (§4), taken after intake on the mind's own reading | Addressing; a perspective is never applied before a task is addressed |

- **A defect of this kind** is a reading from one scope used inside
  another. The instance that defined it: the hypha's `task_address`
  filtered the test inputs by `symbol ≠ 0`, which is a ground and so a
  sensorium reading, and the decoder fixed each task's frame before it
  was addressed, which is a perspective. Both made identity depend on
  perception. Both are removed in track 26 S2.
- **Two minds may perceive one task differently** (another ground,
  another perspective, a vortex held), and the task is still one task:
  its identity belongs to the hypha's scope alone.
- **The court reconstructs a claim on the posted samples.** Where a
  claim states a ground or a perspective, the court reads it as part of
  the claim, never as part of the task (§6.2).

### 6.2 A claim states its ground

A claim (the solve act) states the ground it was spoken under, as an
optional field: one symbol at the source's width, or absent. The field
was added in track 26 S2, inside wire 0x0200's ruled break, with the act
signed as `mycelium solve v2`, and `v3` since S5 carried its outputs as
samples (`dialogue::encode_solve_spoken`,
`SolveAct.ground`). The stated ground is part of the claim, never part of
the task. A tape entry would make it part of the form's identity, which is
why the act carries it instead.

The court reconstructs under the stated ground and never deduces, chooses
or searches for a ground of its own (`cognition::stated_ground`):
- **One symbol stated:** the court reads that symbol's candidacy against
  the task's train pairs, by the three conditions of §3.4, and judges the
  withheld pairs under `Ground::One` of it.
- **Not a candidate:** refused by name, "the stated ground is no
  candidate of the task's ground".
- **Not one symbol:** refused by name, "the stated ground is not one
  symbol of the task".
- **None stated:** the claim is reconstructed under no ground
  (`Ground::None`).

**Speech and the seat's solve call state theirs** (track 26 S5). Each
sentence of a speech act carries an optional stated ground (`Spoken.ground`,
`SpeechAct.grounds`; `carries` bit 2, then `[len u32][symbol]`, never
empty). The court hears each sentence under it (`Court::hear_speech`): the
stated ground is read against the court's copy of the task's family
(`stated_ground`) and opened for the whole adjudication, or the sentence
is refused by name (`REFUSE_GROUND_NO_CANDIDATE`,
`REFUSE_GROUND_NOT_ONE_SYMBOL`). A sentence stating none is heard under no
ground, and a sentence waiting on a cited form keeps the ground it was
heard under for its re-hearing. The court-side deduction on the speech
path is removed: every reader inside the adjudication sees the opened
ground. The mind states the scope open on its thread, else its own
reading of the family it speaks under (§6.1). The seat's solve call
carries the stated ground as a `Bytes` argument between the outputs and
the steps (`seat_mind::task::solve_args`). Claim
`speech_states_its_ground_and_court_reconstructs_under_it`.

**A claim of factorisation states its ground** (track 26 S4c). The act
carries the stated ground as the solve does (`[GRND][len u32][symbol]`).
The court reads it at any rank (`cognition::stated_ground_of`): one
symbol at the samples' width, a candidate by the three conditions, or
refused by the same names. The motion and the residue are read under no
ground, so the stated ground is checked and never changes the residue
derived. Claim `factorisation_claim_states_its_ground`.

**A claim of factorisation states its perspective** (track 26 S6, stage 2).
The act carries the perspective the pair was perceived under after its
residue (`[rank u16][(source u16)(sense u8) · rank][role u8 · rank]`), and
is signed as `mycelium factorisation v2`. The court reads its own copy of
the posted pair through the stated perspective (`Perspective::read`) and
reconstructs there; it never chooses a perspective. One of another rank
than the pair is refused `REFUSE_FACTOR_PERSPECTIVE_RANK`, and one reading
an axis as other than space `REFUSE_FACTOR_PERSPECTIVE_ROLE`, since the
transduction factors every axis as a phase the bodies extend along. The
mind perceives under a perspective (`TaskPercept::under`) and states the
percept's own. The perspective is part of the claim, never of the task.
Claim `claim_states_its_perspective_and_court_reconstructs_under_it`. The
solve of outputs and speech read the alphabet's section in its default
perspective and do not yet state one.

## 7. Who owns a source's code

Source-specific code exists only as a **decoder** from the source's format
to samples. It lives with the source's owner, never in the core library:
- **The house** (or kernel) that posts a corpus's tasks owns that corpus's
  decoder: its file format, its directory layout, its split into train
  and test.
- **Examples and test fixtures** that read a public corpus do so through
  that corpus's decoder, kept outside `src/`. A test of the sensor is a
  claim about samples, written as samples.
- **The core library** (`src/`) contains no file, module, type or
  identifier named for a data source. It contains no constant that is
  true of one source only: a palette size, a ground code, an extent
  limit, a number of axes.

ARC-AGI-2 is one such source. It presents families of rank-2 samples in
pairs, with symbols written `0`–`9`. Its decoder reads its JSON into
samples and stops there: the symbols are labels, and nothing about the
ARC corpus enters the sensor, the mind, the court or the wire.

**Built (track 26, S1).** The decoder is the workspace crate
`sources/arc-agi` (package `mycelium-source-arc-agi`). It depends on the
core; the core never depends on it. It owns:
- the JSON parse (`decode`), with the task as the file gives it. Fixing
  the frame before the task is addressed was the defect of §6.1; S2
  removed it with the former `parse_task`, and no ground is read here;
- the corpus location (`corpus`: `MYCELIUM_CORPUS`, else `data/arc-agi-2`)
  and its split into `training` and `evaluation` (`task_names`);
- the decode to samples (`samples_of`, each placed at `[split, pair,
  member]`);
- the binary `mycelium-arc-agi decode FROM TO [LIMIT]`, which writes the
  **posted task** files;
- the tests that read the corpus.

The posted task (`<name>.task`, `dialogue::encode_posted_task`) is the
source-neutral form a hypha's corpus directory, a client's feed and the
soak read, so none of them parses a dataset's format. Since track 26 S5 it
holds samples of rank `n` (`dialogue::PostedTask`,
`encode_posted_samples`): `[nt u8][(input, output)·nt][nx u8][(input,
[0|1] output?)·nx]`, each sample as the envelope writes it, a withheld
test output absent where the source holds none. It holds decoded values,
exactly. `encode_posted_task` places a raster task's samples, and
`decode_posted_task` reads one back at the raster intake (the soak and a
feed). The hypha reads posted samples whole (`decode_posted_samples`,
track 26 S4c).
