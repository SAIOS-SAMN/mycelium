## 1. What the alphabet is, and why it is fixed

Every motion a mind states (a rule's rotation, a turn of a body, the
orientation of a frame) has to reach a court as an exact object the court
can rebuild and compare by identity. The alphabet is how that is done: a
small set of exact rotations, the **letters**, from which every motion the
language can say is built as a product, a **word**.

Because the letters are fixed and exact, two minds that state the same
motion state the same word, and a court rebuilds the motion from the word
alone. A motion the letters cannot build is not approximated: it is held
as a **remainder** and named (§5).

Three kinds of letter exist, and the distinction matters:

| Kind | What it is | Changes? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seed letters** | The seventeen letters of §2 | Never | **Built** |
| **Admitted letters** | A letter a court opens for a rotation the seed cannot build (§6) | Added by court rulings, never removed | **Built** |
| **Dialect letters** | A letter a mind constructs for itself from what it perceives (§6.3) | Grow in the mind; reach the shared language only by a court ruling | **Built** (client side) |

So the seed never changes, and the language grows by words and by letters
the courts admit.

## 2. The seventeen seed letters

A letter is a **rotor**: an element of the algebra that rotates by being
applied on both sides, `x ↦ R x R̃` (the *sandwich*). A rotor rotates by
twice its own angle. Each letter is stored with its exact inverse, so no
inverse is ever computed.

The seed letters live on a four-axis Euclidean chart with axes
`e0, e1, e2, e3`. Its six coordinate planes are `(0,1) (0,2) (0,3) (1,2)
(1,3) (2,3)`. The **sensor plane** is `(0,1)`: rows of a grid on `e0`,
columns on `e1`.

| Slots | Letter | Planes | What it does on its plane |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | identity | none | nothing |
| 1–6 | `1 + e_ij`, inverse `(1 − e_ij)/2` | all six | a 90° turn |
| 7–12 | `3/5 + 4/5 e_ij` | all six | the rotation by the rational point `(3² − 4², 2·3·4)/5² = (−7, 24)/25` |
| 13–16 | `5/13 + 12/13 e_ij` | `(0,1) (0,2) (0,3) (1,2)` | the rotation by the rational point `(5² − 12², 2·5·12)/13² = (−119, 120)/169` |

A rotation is written by its **rational point** `(cos, sin)` with exact
rational coordinates, never as an angle in decimals. The 90°
letters cover the rotations of the square; the (3,4,5) and (5,12,13)
letters are the rational rotations of infinite order through the primes 5
and 13.

The letters satisfy a fixed set of relations and no others
(`alphabet/letters.rs`, `relators`):
- a 90° turn has order four;
- rotors on one plane commute;
- a 180° turn on a plane sharing one axis with a sensor-plane rotation
  inverts it;
- the (3,4,5) and (5,12,13) letters have infinite order;
- letters on **complementary** planes commute (§7.1); letters on planes
  sharing one axis are never reordered.

Code: `src/transducer/alphabet/letters.rs` (`CAYLEY_ALPHABET_LEN`,
`LetterId`), `rotor.rs`.

## 3. Words: composing letters

A **word** is an ordered tape of letters. Its rotor is the product in tape
order, and the last letter acts first. Familiar motions of a grid are
words, never a table of cases:

| Motion of the grid | Word |
|---|---|
| 90° turn | one 90° letter on the sensor plane |
| 180° turn | two 90° letters on the sensor plane |
| a flip (mirror) of the sensor plane | a 180° turn through a third axis: two 90° letters on plane `(1,2)` |
| a transpose | the product of a 90° turn and a flip |

A flip is not a separate kind of letter. On the sensor plane it reads as a
mirror; in the algebra it is an even rotor through a third axis. Its parity
in a word is the word's **orientation** on the sensor chart.

Code: `alphabet/word.rs`, `alphabet::sheet_word`.

## 4. Spelling a motion

**Spelling** turns a rational similarity of a plane (a rotation, possibly
with a mirror, and a dilation) into a word and an exact dilation:
`alphabet::spell` for the sensor plane, `spell_on` for any coordinate
plane. It is a **peel**: each step removes one layer by an exact reading,
and nothing is tried and checked.

1. **Orientation.** If the motion mirrors the plane, the flip word comes
   off first.
2. **Prime 5.** The rotation's Gaussian integer (its rational point
   written over a common denominator) is divided by `2 + i` as many times
   as it divides exactly: each division is one (3,4,5) letter.
3. **Prime 13.** The same with `3 + 2i`: each division is one (5,12,13)
   letter.
4. **Unit turns.** What is left is a unit (`1, i, −1, −i`): zero to three
   90° letters.
5. **Check.** The spelled word's own rotation is read back on the same
   plane and must equal the motion.

**Example.** The rotation by `(−7, 24)/25` spells as one (3,4,5) letter;
followed by a 90° turn it spells as that letter and one 90° letter. Rebuilding either word from its letters gives back exactly the
rotation it spelled.

Code: `src/transducer/alphabet/spelling.rs` (`spell`, `spell_on`),
`src/transducer/peel.rs` (`peel_nest`).

## 5. Motions that cannot be spelled

A rotation whose Gaussian integer has a prime other than those over 5 and
13, or an odd power of the 180°-turn generator, has no word in the seed.
It is never approximated, rounded or replaced by the nearest letter:
- the connection is not minted (`element::from_connection` answers `None`);
- the mind holds the motion as an open thought, its **remainder**;
- where the motion carries a new prime, the remainder is a
  **`Demand::RotorPrime`** naming the plane, the prime and its witness.

**Example.** The rotation by `(15, 8)/17` has Gaussian integer `4 + i`
over the prime 17. Neither 5 nor 13 divides it, so it does not spell. It
is held as `Demand::RotorPrime { plane: (0, 1), prime: 17, witness: 4 + i }`
(`src/server/cognition/tests/letter.rs`).

## 6. How the letter set grows

### 6.1 Letters admitted by a court (Built)

A court opens a new letter for a prime the seed does not reach:
- the demand carries its prime and a witness rotor;
- the court requires two independent witnesses at one chart, then opens
  the letter `a + b·e_ij` with `a² + b² = p` (for 17: `4 + e_ij`), rules
  it with a receipt, and teaches it through the syllabus;
- the letter is named on a word's tape by its content, never by a slot
  number, so admitted letters never collide;
- the peel gains one layer per admitted prime, in increasing order.

Code: `server/cognition/germinate/listing.rs` (`open_letter`);
refusals for a witness that does not carry its prime in
`court/verdict/replay.rs`.

### 6.2 Rotations off every coordinate plane

A rotor that lies on no coordinate plane (for example
`3 + e₀₁ + e₀₂`, of norm 11) is not spelled and not refused.
`factor_rotor` holds it as `Demand::Standing` with the rotor as witness,
and the court considers it as a candidate generator, admitted only when
two courts reconstruct it (MU-RECURSION 37.7).

### 6.3 Letters of a mind's own dialect (Built, client side)

A mind that meets a residue it cannot say constructs a letter of its own
and adjoins it to its dialect (`construct_and_adjoin`,
`client/sdk/entity/think.rs`; MU-RECURSION 78.109). The letter is the
mind's until a court admits a word built from it; it reaches the shared
language only through that ruling.

### 6.4 Opened axes

When one claim closes on several charts at once, a mind may open an axis:
the transition between the charts is seeded onto a fresh fibre and spoken
up. A court accepts an opened axis only when its fibres are group
elements whose plane block spells (`open_axis`, `court/verdict/`).

## 7. Rotations in four dimensions

### 7.1 Commuting plane pairs

On the four-axis chart, each coordinate plane has a complementary plane
sharing no axis with it, giving three commuting pairs (`BLOCK_ORDER`):
`(0,1)|(2,3)`, `(0,2)|(1,3)`, `(0,3)|(1,2)`. Rotations on complementary
planes commute, so a rotation of four-space factors exactly into one
rotation per plane of a pair, with no correction terms. `factor_rotor`
peels a rotor by these pairs and spells each plane by its own peel.

Code: `src/transducer/alphabet/factor.rs` (`factor_rotor`, `BLOCK_ORDER`).

## 8. The ruled form of a letter (Specified)

The governing specification has ruled what a letter is to become
(MU-RECURSION 78.68, U15). A letter is the triad
`λ = (g_core, ∇B, ΔB)`:
- `g_core`, a conformal transport in `Spin⁺(4,1)(ℚ)`;
- `∇B`, the constructors absorbed into the reservoir;
- `ΔB`, the constructors emitted along null rays.

With `∇B = ΔB = ∅`, a letter is a `Cl(4,1)` versor. The seed letters of §2
are Euclidean rotors on the chart `e0..e3`; carrying them into the triad
is open work, and with ROADMAP track 19 (the conformal algebra
`Cl(n+1,1)(ℚ)` for any `n`) the letters are restated in that algebra. The
seed's meaning (the same seventeen rotations on the same planes) does not
change; the carrier of those rotations does.
