### 0.1 The laws every component keeps

1. **Exact arithmetic.** Values are exact integers or rationals of any
   size, or exact multivectors of Cl(n+1,1)(ℚ). No floating point, in code
   or in tests (`clippy::float_arithmetic` is denied crate-wide).
2. **No scalar selection.** Nothing is chosen by a score, a minimum of a
   functional, or a magnitude. Where a rule needs an order, the rule names
   the coordinate and the order, and a tie is held whole or refused by
   name (Concordance 1.10; `clause.rs`, `Tie`).
3. **No voting and no weights.** Agreement is algebraic (second vantage,
   47.3); nothing is decided by a count of votes or a weighted sum.
4. **Hashes as transport only.** A digest may compact coordinates held
   losslessly elsewhere; it is never an object's identity. Identity is by
   decoded coordinates.
5. **Named refusals.** Every input a component cannot accept is refused
   with a reason the caller can act on; nothing is dropped silently, and
   nothing aborts (`unwrap_used`, `panic` and the lints beside them are denied).
6. **Tests as claims by the algebra.** A refusal by name, a fixed locus, a
   replay or reconstruction, or an exact identity (MU-STANDARDS 3.2). No
   golden bytes (Concordance 1.4), no census diffs, no thresholds.
7. **Bounded by strokes.** Work that can grow with its input runs under the
   stroke budget and is refused by name (`Unbounded`) or left unread when
   the budget is spent, never run on.

### 3.1 Noise XX encrypted transport

**What it is.** The TCP connection between any two parties (hypha and
hypha, client and hypha) wrapped in a Noise protocol session with the
`Noise_XX_25519_ChaChaPoly_BLAKE2s` handshake. Each host holds one static
Curve25519 key. The tunnel gives confidentiality and integrity of the
bytes in flight. Identity stays where it is: in the Ed25519 signatures on
acts, verified on admission.

**The gap.** In release 0.2.0, acts travel over plain TCP. The server door
(`src/bin/mycelium-server/transport.rs`, `read_frame`) and the SDK session
(`src/client/sdk/session.rs`, `src/anastome/dialogue/chatter.rs`) read frames
from a `TcpStream` directly. `src/network/` holds `mesh.rs`, `nerve/` and
`wire.rs`, and no encrypted channel. ROADMAP track 5 lists "an encrypted
transport" as work to do. README › *Node capabilities in 0.2.0* records
the transport as Ed25519-signed acts over TCP.

**Design.**
- A `NoiseStream` in `src/network/noise.rs` implements `Read` and
  `Write`, so the frame codec (`network::wire`) runs over it unchanged.
- The handshake is XX: both static keys are transmitted encrypted, and
  neither side needs the other's key in advance.
- **Trust on first use.** The host's static key and its pins are kept in
  the home (`noise.key`, `noise.pins`). The first static key seen for a
  peer is pinned. A later handshake presenting a different key for a
  pinned peer is refused by name (`PeerKeyChanged`, carrying the pinned
  and the presented keys) and never silently re-pinned. Re-pinning is an
  operator act.
- **Identity is not moved into the tunnel.** A Noise static key
  authenticates a channel, never a voice or a hypha. Admission still
  verifies every act's Ed25519 signature; a valid tunnel carrying an
  unsigned or mis-signed act is refused as it is today.
- **Framing inside the tunnel.** Noise messages are bounded at 65535 bytes
  including the 16-byte tag; a frame is carried in chunks of at most
  65519 bytes of plaintext, and the frame budget (`BODY_BUDGET`) is
  checked on the reassembled length before any body is allocated.
- **Opt-in first.** The transport is selected per peer address. Plain TCP
  remains for one-host development until multi-host deployment (track 5)
  makes the encrypted channel the default.
- **Named refusals** (`NoiseRefusal`) for a failed handshake, a peer that
  presents no static key, a changed static key, a message past the Noise
  bound, a frame past the budget, a decryption failure and a malformed key
  or pin store. None is a panic.

**Adaptations from the precursor.** The predecessor implementation held
the transport in its SDK and recovered a missing remote static key as a
zero key. Mycelium places the stream in `network`, where both the server
door and the SDK reach it, and refuses a handshake that yields no remote
static key. The predecessor's `expect` on the handshake pattern is a
constructor that refuses instead.

**Acceptance claims.**
- A frame written on one side of an XX session is read whole on the other
  (replay; built as `initiate_completes_handshake_and_round_trips_frames`).
- A changed static key on a pinned host is refused by name (built as
  `pin_refuses_changed_peer_key`).
- The first static key seen for a peer is the pinned one: a fixed locus
  across a reconnect.
- A frame past the budget is refused by name at the reassembled length,
  before its body is held (built as
  `write_frame_refuses_over_budget_before_sending` and
  `read_frame_refuses_head_past_budget`).
- A signed act is admitted through the tunnel and an unsigned one is not:
  the tunnel adds nothing to identity.

**Track** 5. **Effort** medium. **Status** In progress: being built as
`src/network/noise.rs`; wiring into the server door and the SDK session,
and the pin store in the home, may remain after the first commit.

### 3.2 Mutation audit

**What it is.** A standing check that the tests guard the laws they
claim to guard. A catalogue of mutants is kept: each one names a law, the
file, the exact source span that implements the law, the replacement that
breaks it, and the narrowest test that must fail when it is broken. The
audit applies each mutant in a scratch worktree, runs that test, and
reports a verdict per mutant.

**The gap.** In release 0.2.0, the gate (`xtask/main.rs`, `xtask/lint/`) takes three
readings: the reading bound (`length.rs`), the Euclidean vocabulary
(`euclidean.rs`) and clippy (`warnings.rs`). Nothing in it checks that a
law's breakage is detected by a test. `proptest` is a dev-dependency and
is not yet used by any test. MU-STANDARDS §3 states tests as claims, and
nothing measures whether each claim can fail.

**Design.**
- `cargo run -- mutate` in `xtask` reads the catalogue (a text file kept
  beside the gate, one entry per mutant).
- Each mutant: `law` (the specification section it implements), `file`,
  the exact original span, the replacement, and `test` (a module path).
- The audit creates a scratch worktree of the current commit, applies one
  mutant at a time by exact span replacement, runs only the named test,
  and restores the span. The working tree is never modified.
- **Verdicts, three and exact:**
  - `killed`: the named test failed with the mutant applied;
  - `survived`: the named test passed with the mutant applied (a law no
    test notices; a defect in the tests);
  - `stale`: the original span is not found in the file (the code moved;
    the entry is re-anchored, never counted as either of the others).
- The audit reports every verdict and exits non-zero on any `survived` or
  `stale` entry. It is run before a release and on a change to a file the
  catalogue names; it is not part of every gate run, because each mutant
  is a build.
- Builds run under the gate's own target directory, so the audit never
  disturbs a running build.

**Adaptations from the precursor.** The earlier settlement-layer
prototype kept mutants in two scripts run from the workspace root on a
clean tree, reverting through the version-control tool. Mycelium keeps
the catalogue as data, runs it from the gate binary in a scratch
worktree, and adds the `stale` verdict so a moved span is reported rather
than read as a surviving law.

**Acceptance claims.**
- A mutant whose test fails is `killed`, and one whose test passes has
  `survived`, on a fixture catalogue against a fixture crate.
- A span not in the file is `stale`: a refusal by name, never counted as
  killed.
- The working tree is unchanged after an audit: a fixed locus.
- A first catalogue covering at least the treasury audit
  (`Treasury::audit`), canonical decoding of one act, second-vantage
  equality (`same_vertex`) and a clause's tie rule, every entry `killed`.

**Track** 14 (and MU-STANDARDS §4, the gate). **Effort** medium.
**Status** In progress: the `mutate` subcommand is being added to `xtask`.

### 3.3 Named refusals in `learn_form`

**What it is.** A defect fix in the mind's vocabulary. When a mind learns
a form (`Presentation::learn_form`), every vertex it does not file is
reported with the reason.

**The gap.** In release 0.2.0, `src/transducer/sentence/presentation.rs`,
`learn_form` (and its sibling `file_form`), returns the count of vertices
filed. A vertex with no motion, a motion whose modes need cannot be read
(`cga::modes_need` returns `None`), and a motion whose coordinates fail to
read under the budget (`Coordinates::of` returns `Err`) are each skipped
by `continue`, with no reason returned. A form is partly learned and the
caller cannot tell which vertices were dropped, or why.

**Design.**
- Learning a form returns a `Learned` account in which every vertex of
  the form stands in exactly one of three places: filed (by address),
  refused (by its coordinate in the form, its address and a named reason),
  or not a word (a frame, a boundary or a demand, which carries no motion
  to file).
- The reasons are named (`Unlearned`): no member to read a motion off, no
  base action at the member's carriage, a motion that is no versor or
  whose modes' stroke is past the carrier, coordinates that did not read
  (carrying the reader's own reason), a hue listing that sends one hue two
  ways, and an inert vertex that moves and recolours nothing.
- A refusal is held at the key the vertex would have been filed at, so one
  key is held or refused, never both; filing the vertex later clears it.
- Callers that only counted now read the account; none discards the
  refusals silently.

**Adaptations.** None from a precursor; the fix applies the
named-refusal law to a site that predates it.

**Acceptance claims.**
- An inert vertex is refused by name.
- A vertex whose coordinates do not read carries the reader's reason.
- A refused vertex filed later leaves no refusal at its key: a fixed locus.
- Every vertex of a form is filed, refused or not a word: the three sets
  partition the form's vertices (in part built as
  `learn_form_files_readable_form` and
  `learn_form_refuses_unreadable_word`).

**Track** 7. **Effort** small. **Status** In progress (the reasons above
are those being built; the final names are the code's).

### 3.4 Integer homology and invariant factors

**What it is.** Two exact normal forms Mycelium lacks:
1. **Smith normal form over ℤ.** For an integer matrix `A`, unimodular
   `U` and `V` with `U A V = D`, `D` diagonal with invariant factors
   `d₁ | d₂ | … | d_r`. From it: the rank, the torsion of a chain
   complex's homology, and whether an integer chain with a given boundary
   exists. A chain can be solvable over ℚ and not over ℤ; the Smith form
   decides which, and constructs the integer chain when it exists.
2. **Rational canonical form over ℚ[x].** For a linear function `f`, the
   invariant factors `p₁ | p₂ | … | p_k` of `xI − f` over ℚ[x], whose
   product is the characteristic polynomial. Two linear functions are
   similar over ℚ exactly when the invariant factors of the two agree.

**The gap.**
- No Smith normal form, invariant factor or elementary divisor appears in
  `src/`. `src/core/exponents.rs` holds the Hermite normal form with its
  transform (`hnf_with_transform`, `reduce_against`): exact over ℤ, it
  decides membership in a lattice and gives the factorization, but it does
  not give torsion or the diagonal form.
- `src/transducer/cga/linear.rs` holds `LinearFunction`, which reads the
  characteristic polynomial (Newton's identities over power-sum traces),
  the kernel and a polynomial in `f`. It does not read invariant factors.
- `src/transducer/cga/modes.rs`, `Undecomposed::Irrational(usize)`,
  refuses a generator whose characteristic polynomial has a factor with no
  rational root, naming only that factor's **degree**. The refusal cannot
  name the factor itself, and two different irreducible factors of one
  degree read as one refusal.

**Design.**
- **Smith form** in `src/core/` beside `exponents.rs`, over `BigInt` of
  any size, on **sparse** rows (only nonzero entries held). Returns `D`
  with `U` and `V`, because `U` and `V` are the provenance: which chains
  and which cells each invariant factor came from.
- **Integer solvability.** `solve_over_z(∂, b)` returns the integer chain,
  or a named refusal `SolvableOverQOnly { factor }` naming the invariant
  factor that obstructs it, or `NoSolution`. A chain homologous over ℚ but
  not over ℤ is named, never rounded.
- **Homology.** For a declared complex, `H_k`'s free rank
  (`n_k − rank ∂_k − rank ∂_{k+1}`) and its torsion coefficients (the
  invariant factors of `∂_{k+1}` greater than 1), held as exact integers.
- **Invariant factors over ℚ[x]** for `LinearFunction`, computed from the
  linear function's images by polynomial Smith reduction over ℚ[x]. With
  them, `Undecomposed::Irrational` carries the irreducible factor's exact
  coefficients, not only its degree.
- **No digest identity.** A similarity class is held as its invariant
  factors, decoded and compared as polynomials. A digest of them may index
  a book; it never decides equality.
- **Budgeted.** Every reduction step spends strokes; a reduction the
  budget does not finish is refused as `Unbounded(size)`, never run on.

**What it serves.**
- **Track 18:** certificates over ℤ. An agreement's cocycle may be a
  coboundary over ℚ and not over ℤ; the torsion names the obstruction
  exactly.
- **Track 19:** kinds of bond. Similarity of the bond versors' linear
  functions is **necessary, not sufficient**, for conjugacy by a versor of
  the pole algebra: two bonds with different invariant factors are of
  different kinds, and two with equal factors are then tested for versor
  conjugacy. The residue of a closed chain of bonds is read as torsion
  (G4).
- **Track 12, MU-SCIENCE V1:** homology computations and integer
  solvability as certificate checks (§3.5).

**Adaptations from the precursors.** The earlier settlement-layer
prototype computed Smith form on dense matrices and its homology with two
full decompositions per degree; Mycelium's is sparse, and reads a rank
without the full transform where only the rank is needed (the split of
homology into its free and torsion parts as two readings). The
convergence-engine computed the rational canonical form of dense matrices
and identified a similarity class by a Keccak digest of the encoded
factors; Mycelium keeps the factors as the class and acts on
`LinearFunction` images, with no matrix type.

**Acceptance claims.**
- Each invariant factor divides the next: an exact identity on `D`.
- `U A V = D` with `U` and `V` unimodular: reconstruction, and `det U`,
  `det V` are ±1.
- The product of the invariant factors is the characteristic polynomial:
  an exact identity over ℚ[x], against `LinearFunction::characteristic`.
- Invariant factors are unchanged under versor conjugation: a fixed locus,
  for `f` and `V f V⁻¹` over versors of Cl(4,1)(ℚ).
- A chain solvable over ℚ but not over ℤ is named: a refusal by name, on a
  boundary matrix with torsion 2.
- The torsion of the real projective plane is 2: an exact identity on a
  minimal triangulation of ℝP².
- An irrational mode names its irreducible factor: a refusal by name,
  replacing the degree-only reading.
- A reduction past its budget is refused as `Unbounded`.

**Track** 18, 19 and 12. **Effort** medium. **Status** Specified.

### 3.5 Chain-complex checker as a V1 certificate kind

**What it is.** A certificate kind for MU-SCIENCE V1 whose geometry is
data. A claim declares a finite chain complex (cell counts `n₀ … n_K` and
sparse exact boundary maps `∂_k : C_k → C_{k−1}`) and a witness chain. The
court checks two things and nothing else: the declaration is a complex,
and the witness closes.

**The gap.** MU-SCIENCE V1 lists `lp-optimum`, `sat`, `unsat`,
`factorization` and `isomorphism`. The verifier interface (MU-SCIENCE
§9.1) is specified and is not yet in the court (track 12, S1). The
Čech machinery in `src/transducer/cech/` (`complex.rs`, `coboundary.rs`)
builds complexes from a mind's charts; it does not admit a complex
declared by a claim.

**Design.**
- **Admission** decodes the declaration canonically (entries sorted by
  cell, nonzero coefficients, no duplicates) and refuses any other
  encoding by name before escrow (MU-SCIENCE §4.1).
- **Check (i):** `∂_k ∘ ∂_{k+1} = 0` for every `k`, by exact sparse
  multiplication. A failure is refused as `NotAComplex { degree }`.
- **Check (ii):** `∂_k c = 0` for the witness `c`. A failure is refused as
  `OpenBoundary { cell }`, naming the first `(k−1)`-cell whose signed flux
  does not cancel.
- **Optional check (iii):** with §3.4, the witness's class in `H_k` over ℤ,
  and whether two witnesses are homologous (the difference of the two a
  boundary).
- **Fuel is court strokes.** Every multiplication and every cancellation
  spends from the court's stroke budget; an unfinished check is unread and
  resumes on the next send.
- **Identity by decoded coordinates.** Two claims are the same claim when
  the claims' decoded complexes and witnesses are equal as structures; a digest
  of the encoding compacts it for transport only.
- **Payment through POW++.** The verdict carries the reconstructed object
  and its discharge, and settles through the existing lanes (POUW for the
  closure, POWV for the verifying courts, POWC for a convergence across
  derivations). There is no credit formula over cell counts or degrees.

**Adaptations from the precursor.** The earlier settlement-layer
prototype implemented the same two checks with fuel as an internal
counter, identified work by its canonical bytes, and priced it by a
formula of its own. Mycelium keeps the two checks and the checks' refusal names,
spends court strokes, identifies by decoded structure, and pays only
through the POW++ arms.

**Acceptance claims.**
- A declaration whose boundaries do not compose to zero is refused as not a
  complex.
- An open witness names its leaking cell.
- A Kirchhoff cycle on a ring closes: a 1-cycle on an `n`-ring, exact.
- A non-canonical declaration is refused before escrow.
- Two encodings of one complex are one claim: reconstruction by decoded
  coordinates.
- A check past its budget is unread and resumes on the next send.

**Track** 12 (S2). **Effort** medium. **Status** Specified.

### 3.6 Fault-injection campaign and invariant auditor

**What it is.** A harness that runs a mesh of hyphae in one process (or on
one host), injects named faults on chosen topologies, and audits exact
invariants after every campaign. A campaign is reproducible from a printed
seed: the mesh size, the topology, the fault list and the seed are its
whole description.

**The gap.** `tests/` holds two integration suites (`alpha_plan.rs`,
`g_cga.rs`). The court's equivocation proof is tested in
`court/admission/tests/equivocation.rs`, and the treasury audit
(`Treasury::audit`) is checked from the rows. No harness composes faults
across several courts and audits the invariants mesh-wide.
`mycelium-soak` (`src/bin/mycelium-soak/`) is a load and durability run,
not a fault campaign.

**Design.**
- **Ten fault classes,** each a first-class type with its own injector:

  | Fault | What it injects |
  |---|---|
  | `InvalidSignature` | An act whose Ed25519 signature does not verify. |
  | `Replay` | A valid signed act sent twice. |
  | `DoubleClaimRace` | One work claimed at two courts across a partition. |
  | `EquivocatingLedger` | Two signed receipts from one key at one position. |
  | `SybilSwarm` | Many unbonded voices from one operator. |
  | `RespellRaceSaturation` | Concurrent re-expressions (POWR) of one word. |
  | `DrainedPurseDraw` | A draw or lease against a balance already spent. |
  | `RestoreFromSnapshotSigner` | An honest node restored from a stale snapshot, re-signing a consumed position. |
  | `MalformedName` | A well-signed act whose content the court's content law refuses. |
  | `GerminalFlood` | A flood of germinal words at one court. |

- **Four topologies:** a full battery (every fault on one mesh), a clean
  split, rolling isolation, and an asymmetric partition.
- **Exact invariants,** audited after every campaign:
  1. **Conservation by ledger replay:** every node's balances, bonds and
     holds re-derive from its own ledger rows, and
     `supply = genesis + mint − burn = Σbalance + Σbond + Σheld` holds per
     lane (`Treasury::audit`).
  2. **Once per work across the mesh:** each work is paid once; a node
     that diverged on it holds the receipt that annuls the race.
  3. **Every burn revokes:** each burn for a proven violation pairs with
     the revocation of the position it burned.
  4. **No refusal without a receipt:** every refused act, at the signature
     gate and at content law, leaves a receipt.
- **The auditor keeps its own view.** Where an invariant compares an
  accused node's account with another's, the auditor reads the accused's
  counterpart directly, never through the accused. A reading routed
  through the accused lets a lie cancel out of the check.
- **Concurrent faults target separate nodes.** Faults in one campaign are
  scheduled on disjoint nodes, so no fault breaks another's precondition.
- **Typed readings, never log text.** The auditor reads ledger rows and
  receipts as decoded values through the treasury and the receipt chain.
- **Soak to dry.** The campaign runner enumerates every (topology, fault
  subset) up to a size and reports a violation with its seed; each
  violation becomes a regression claim.

**Adaptations from the precursor.** The predecessor implementation
defined these fault classes, ran them on a twelve-node mesh with fixed
test keys, and audited its treasury by parsing ledger lines as text and
comparing lines byte for byte. Mycelium rewrites the harness against its
current admission (`src/court/admission/`) and treasury
(`src/economy/treasury.rs`), reads rows as decoded values, and compares
accounts by decoded value. The two lessons the predecessor's own runs taught (the
auditor's independent view, and disjoint targets) are kept as design
rules.

**Acceptance claims.**
- One claim per fault class, each stating what the court must do, for
  example: a replayed act moves value once; an equivocating ledger
  forfeits the whole bond; a restored signer re-signing a consumed
  position is named; a malformed name leaves a receipt.
- Conservation holds after every campaign: an exact identity per lane.
- A campaign reproduces from its printed seed: replay.
- A lie routed through the accused is still detected: the auditor's
  independent view.

**Track** 14. **Effort** large. **Status** Specified.

### 3.7 Malformed-input corpus; refuse and wait

**What it is.** A corpus of byte strings every Anastome decoder must
refuse, kept as regression claims on the decoded result, and a typed
distinction between two outcomes a stream reader meets: **refuse** (the
bytes can never become a valid act) and **wait** (a valid act has begun
and not finished).

**The gap.** The act decoders in `src/anastome/dialogue/` return `Option`:
a truncated payload and a malformed one are both `None`. At the server
door, `read_frame` reads a five-byte header and a body of the declared
length, and refuses a declared length past `BODY_BUDGET` plus the point's
length with `REFUSE_MALFORMED` before reading the body. Codec hardening is
specified in track 14 item 2, including corpora of malformed tapes; none
is in the tree.

**Design.**
- A decode outcome type with three cases: an act, `Refuse { why }` and
  `Wait { need }`, where `need` is the exact count of bytes still owed.
- **An exact length bound.** A declared length equal to the bound is
  waited for; one byte past it is refused at the header, before any body
  is held.
- **The corpus** holds must-refuse cases only: a non-reduced rational, a
  zero denominator, a sign byte outside its range, a length past the
  bound, a duplicate or unsorted entry, a trailing byte, an unknown layout
  version. Each case is a claim that decoding yields `Refuse` with the
  named reason.
- **Accept cases are never golden bytes** (Concordance 1.4). An accepted
  act is tested by decode∘encode equality on decoded values, and by
  differential round-trip (track 14 item 2), never by comparing its
  encoding with a stored file.

**Adaptations from the precursor.** The earlier settlement-layer
prototype's conformance corpus held accept, refuse, skip and wait cases as
stored byte files, checked against a manifest. Mycelium keeps the refuse
and wait cases as decoded claims and drops the stored accept bytes. The
`skip` verdict belongs to an open record stream; Anastome is a closed set
of acts, and `skip` is not adopted.

**Acceptance claims.**
- A ratio not in lowest terms is refused, never repaired.
- A length at the bound waits, and one byte past it is refused.
- A truncated act waits for exactly the bytes it owes.
- Every corpus case is refused with its named reason.

**Track** 14 (codec hardening). **Effort** small. **Status** Specified.

### 3.8 Rule-language extensions

**What it is.** Five additions to the clause vocabulary that MU-COGNITION
§1.3 names as the road, plus clauses learnable as vocabulary. Each changes
the form language and the court's replay, so each is a court change,
built with its codec, its replay and its claims.

**The current vocabulary** (`src/transducer/sentence/form/clause.rs`):
predicates `Pred::{All, Hue, Chart, Cmp, Rank, Touches, Not, And, Any}`
over coordinates `Coord::{Shell, Height, Width, Top, Left, Bottom, Right,
Hues, Contacts, Enclosed, Tally, Peers, Radius, Lane}`, with ties named by
`Tie::{Hold, Refuse, Then}`; acts `Act::{Recolour, Carry, Fill, Extend,
Copy, Crop, Scale, Parts, Connect, Echo, Panes, Tile, Bend, Slide,
Repaint}`; hues `Paint::{Own, Hue, Of}`. The gaps below are read against
it.

**(a) Act parameters measured per pair.**
- *Gap:* `Act::Scale { up, down }` and `Act::Tile { rows, cols, turns }`
  carry constants; `Act::Carry` and `Act::Echo` carry one fixed motion. A
  family whose scale, tiling or translation differs pair to pair is not
  stated by one clause.
- *Design:* a parameter may be a measured hole, naming the measurement it
  reads on each pair: the ratio of the output frame to the input frame
  (scale, tile, reduce), or the translation between a selected body and
  its image. The measurement is exact; where it is not (a ratio that is
  not an integer for `Scale`, frames that are not commensurate), the
  clause is refused by name on that pair. Where two readings of the
  parameter are possible on a pair, the clause is refused, never broken
  by a silent tie.
- *Claims:* a scale by the frames' ratio holds where the ratio differs
  pair to pair; a non-integer frame ratio is refused by name.

**(b) A template placed over a lattice.**
- *Gap:* `Act::Copy { template }` places one template centre onto centre
  on each selected body. A template repeated over a lattice of positions
  (a skew lattice included) is not stated.
- *Design:* the lattice is read from the placement differences of the
  witnessed copies as the differences' Hermite normal form (`core::exponents`,
  exact over ℤ at any size), and its offset is anchored to a body a
  predicate selects. Membership of a position is decided by reduction
  against the form. No candidate positions are enumerated; a single
  witnessed copy mints no lattice and is refused by name.
- *Claims:* a skew lattice is read whole from its placement differences;
  a lone witness mints no lattice.

**(c) First contact as a relation, and a slide toward a partner.**
- *Gap:* `Pred::Touches` reads contact that already holds; `Act::Slide`
  carries a body along a fixed unit ray to the border or the first painted
  cell. A body's first-met partner along each ray, and a slide toward that
  partner, are not stated.
- *Design:* `Pred::FirstMet(Box<Pred>)` selects the bodies whose first
  contact along a ray is a body the inner predicate selects;
  `Act::Slide { toward: Pred, stop }` carries each selected body along the
  ray to its partner. Partners met at one distance are held whole (every
  one of them is the partner), never reduced to one. Where the ray to the
  partner is not a lattice unit, the slide is refused by name. The grid
  has a border: there is no toroidal wrap.
- *Claims:* a body slides to its first-met partner; partners at one
  distance are held whole; a ray that is not a lattice unit is refused.

**(d) Colour-role equivariance.**
- *Gap:* `Act::Recolour` and the gauges of `Carry` and `Echo` are fixed hue
  maps. A rule that holds across palettes (the same roles in different
  hues) is stated once per palette.
- *Design:* a scene's content is read up to its hue permutation as its
  gauge-orbit class, carrying the trivialization so the class re-inflates
  to the exact scene. A rule stated on the class is applied on each pair
  through that pair's trivialization. An equivariance check reads whether
  a rule's image map commutes with the hue permutation and, where it does
  not, reports the first site where commutation breaks: a residue, never
  a boolean alone. A rule that names an absolute hue is gauge-fixed, and
  the check says so.
- *Claims:* the class quotient re-inflates to the scene; an equivariant
  rule holds under every palette of its class; a gauge-fixed rule names
  where commutation breaks.

**(e) Hue as a table over a named measured coordinate.**
- *Gap:* `Paint::Of(Pred)` reads a hue off a touched partner. A hue that
  depends on an exact coordinate of the body itself (its size, its
  contact count, its rank) is not stated.
- *Design:* `Paint::Table { coord: Coord, rows: Vec<(Value, u8)> }`: the
  rule names the coordinate and the table; a body whose coordinate value
  is off the table is refused by name, never given a default. Counts and
  ranks enter only as predicates or table keys the rule names, with ties
  as named (Concordance 1.10).
- *Claims:* a hue table paints by the named coordinate; a value off the
  table is refused.

**Clauses learnable as vocabulary.**
- *Gap:* `Presentation` (`src/transducer/sentence/presentation.rs`)
  learns vertices (`learn_form`) and letters (`learn_letter`); it does not
  file a clause as a learned word. A clause a mind found closes its family
  and is spoken again only by re-reading it.
- *Design:* a clause the court admits (second vantage, 47.3) is filed in
  the presentation at its address with its admission, and is spoken on a
  later family by citation. An unadmitted clause stays in the mind's
  dialect.
- *Claims:* an admitted clause is spoken by citation; an unadmitted
  clause stays in the dialect.

**Adaptations from the precursor.** ipsal-autic, the language project the
alphabet came from, held measured holes (including hues named by majority
or minority), lattice placement with a phase anchored to a minimal origin,
toroidal first contact and the class quotient. In Mycelium: a hue named by
a count is a `Rank` over a named coordinate with its tie named; an anchor
is a body a predicate selects, never a minimal origin; first contact stops
at the border; the lattice is read in `BigInt` through the existing
Hermite form, not in fixed-width integers; and every addition is judged by
closure on families it did not see, never by the task it closed.

**Track** 7. **Effort** large (five court changes, each one phase).
**Status** Specified.

### 3.9 Body fate precondition; the representation functor

**What it is.** Two pieces of the family reader's mathematics:
1. **Body fate as a typed precondition.** Before a rule is extracted from
   a family, each pair's bodies are each given a fate (carried, created,
   vanished, deformed, gathered), and the family is read only where the
   fates are consistent across its pairs. Where they are not, extraction
   is refused with the pair and the body that breaks consistency named.
2. **The representation functor.** Chains of movements are represented as
   transports such that adding chains corresponds exactly to composing
   the chains' transports. It turns a question about composition into one about
   addition, which the integer machinery of §3.4 answers.

**The gap.** `src/transducer/family/flux.rs` reads each percept's
non-motion changes (creation, vanishing, deformation, a source of
multiplicity) and puts them to the law register's continuity law
(`laws::verify_flux`); its header records that holding the fluxes across a
family's percepts is the next faculty. Extraction
(`src/transducer/family/extract.rs`) has no typed fate precondition: a
family whose pairs disagree on a body's fate reaches extraction and is
refused, if at all, by the remainder it leaves. No functor from chains to
transports is in `src/transducer/`.

**Design.**
- `Fate` per body per pair, read from the percept's links; a family
  precondition `fates_agree(family) -> Result<Fates, FateRefusal>` with
  named refusals (`FateDiffers { pair, body }`,
  `MassNotConserved { pair }` from the continuity law).
- The functor: a chain `Σ c_i (site_i, direction_i)` is sent to a
  transport in a square-zero ideal, so that the transport of `a + b` is
  the composite of the transports of `a` and `b`. Exact over ℚ; the
  identity is checked, never assumed.
- For track 18, the functor carries an agreement's chains to transports,
  and §3.4's Smith form reads the class of the result over ℤ.

**Adaptations from the precursor.** ipsal-autic built the representation
functor on its own polynomial embedding with a fixed chart linearization.
Mycelium builds it on its versors and `LinearFunction`, and records the
chart's linearization as a coordinate of the result, not as a constant.

**Acceptance claims.**
- A family whose pairs disagree on a fate is refused, naming the body.
- A family with consistent fates passes the precondition unchanged: a
  fixed locus.
- Chains add exactly as the chains' transports compose: an exact identity.
- The product of two members of the ideal is zero: an exact identity.

**Track** 7 (fate precondition) and 18 (functor). **Effort** medium.
**Status** Specified.

### 3.10 SDK depths and a file-feed door

**What it is.** A statement of the SDK's depths, so a developer finds the
shallowest one that serves, and a door that turns task-shaped files into
claims without code.

| Depth | What a developer brings | What runs |
|---|---|---|
| **Depth 0: run a node** | Nothing but a host | `mycelium-server`; earns POWV for verification (README › *Running a node*). |
| **Depth 1: bring tasks** | Task-shaped files | The file-feed door submits each file as one claim. |
| **Depth 2: bring a solver** | A program that states rules | A client on `mycelium-sdk` (`sdk/examples/client.rs`). |
| **Depth 3: bring physics** | A universe's law set | A kernel that signs the Concordance and seals a physics profile (MU-SCIENCE §3.2; MU-HOSTING §3.1). |

**The gap.** Depths 0 and 2 exist (the server; the SDK and its example).
No door reads files into claims; a developer with tasks and no solver
writes a client. The depths are not stated in the SDK's documentation.

**Design.**
- **The door** is a small client on `sdk::{Voice, Session}`: it watches
  one directory, decodes each file dropped there as a task-shaped object
  (an ARC-format task, or a claim kind once track 12 S1 lands), and
  submits it under the operator's voice. **One file, one claim.**
- Each file is answered by a file beside it carrying the decoded verdict
  or the named refusal. A file that does not decode is refused by name and
  never retried silently.
- The door holds no state but the voice key; a restart re-reads the
  directory and resumes by asking the court, never by a local ledger.
- The door speaks only through the SDK surface (§4), so it is also that
  surface's first external consumer.

**Adaptations from the precursor.** The predecessor implementation's
uplink door read JSON files, expected every step to succeed, and hosted a
kernel-side install path in which one kernel seconded its own offers.
Mycelium's door refuses by name at every step, and never seconds: install
remains the second vantage's (47.3).

**Acceptance claims.**
- A file dropped in the feed is one claim, answered beside it.
- A file that does not decode is refused by name.
- A restarted door resumes by asking the court: replay.

**Track** 9 (depths in the SDK documentation) and 15 (the door, on the
operator's path, MU-HOSTING §2). **Effort** medium. **Status** Specified.

### 3.12 World-model onboarding reference

**What it is.** A checklist for MU-HOSTING §3.1 step 4 ("declare the state
space exactly"), drawn from a widely used world-model interface: the
Gymnasium-style spaces such libraries declare for observations and actions
(discrete, multi-discrete, box, image and dictionary spaces), and the interface's
episode protocol (reset with a seed, step, close).

**The gap.** MU-HOSTING §3.1 step 4 requires that states cross the boundary
as exact coordinates under the profile's declared unit map, and gives no
mapping from the spaces world models already declare.

**Design** (a table in MU-HOSTING §3.1):

| World-model declaration | Mycelium's exact declaration |
|---|---|
| Discrete space of `n` values | A coordinate in `0 … n−1`, exact integer |
| Multi-discrete space | One integer coordinate per axis, each with its bound |
| Box with float `low` and `high` | Rational bounds and a unit map: each axis an exact rational in `[low, high]` with a declared denominator; a value with no exact representation does not cross |
| Image (RGB box) | A grid of exact integer channels, or a hue table |
| Dictionary of spaces | A named product of the above |
| Episode reset with a seed | Founding from a seed recorded in the domain; reconstruction by rebuilding from it (MU-HOSTING §7) |
| Step | One reported transition, checked by the profile's laws (track 15, H3) |

**Track** 15 (H3). **Effort** small. **Status** Specified.

ROADMAP track 9 asks for a stable, versioned client surface narrower than
the crate's public API. This section is the design of it.

### 4.2 Design

- **A crate of its own.** `mycelium-sdk`, a workspace member beside
  `mycelium` and `forms`, re-exporting a narrow surface. It holds no
  protocol logic of its own: every item is the library's, so the server
  and the SDK cannot drift.
- **The surface, by area:**

  | Area | Items |
  |---|---|
  | Identity | Voice key load and mint; signing |
  | Session | `Voice`, `Session` (open, speak, keyed speech) |
  | Tasks and solves | Task request and envelope; solve with rule; verdict and its codes |
  | Rule seam | Extraction, minting and speaking a rule; typed steps (`rule::sentence`); rule elements and clauses |
  | Seats | Taking a seat in a house; the seated mind's senses |
  | Wallet and leases | Wallet reading; lease open, reply and close |
  | Readings | The typed readings a client may ask a hypha for |
  | A kernel's house | Domain founding, the universe, the endowment, the house side of seats, laws verify, the clock, the Concordance signature, framing (`house`) |

- **Semantic versioning separate from the server.** `mycelium-sdk`
  carries its own version. A change to any re-exported item's signature
  or behaviour is a major version in 1.x and a minor version in 0.x; the
  server may change freely behind it.
- **A wire compatibility statement.** Each SDK release names the Anastome
  act layouts it speaks (each act's layout version where one is defined)
  and the server releases that answer them, in its CHANGELOG entry. Where
  the wire gains a single protocol version, the statement cites it.
- **Minimal dependencies and features.** The SDK depends on the library
  with server-only parts disabled: `mycelium` gains a default-on `server`
  feature holding the allocator, its statistics, signal handling and file
  watching, and the library modules that use them. The SDK's own features:
  - `noise`: the encrypted transport (§3.1);
  - `rules`: the rule seam (on by default; a wallet-only client turns it
    off);
  - `seats`: seats and the seated mind.
- **Documentation on docs.rs.** Every re-exported item documented, with
  the depths (§3.10) on the crate's front page, and each example linked.
- **Examples per POW++ arm:** POUW (task, rule, solve: today's
  `client` example), POWC (a rule citing installed words), POWR (a rewrite
  spoken through `Session::speak`), a lease (open, pay, close), and a seat.
  POWV is earned by running a node and is documented as Depth 0.
- **Publication to crates.io** under Apache-2.0. crates.io requires every
  dependency to be published, so `mycelium` (the library, with its
  binaries) and `forms` are published at matching versions with the SDK.
- **A client conformance read.** A machine-readable statement of the acts
  a client must speak (with the acts' layouts) and the readings it must
  answer, served by a hypha and kept in the repository, with a conformance
  suite any client runs against a local network. This is the published
  conformance read of MU-HOSTING §2.5 item 4 and track 15 H1. Conformance
  is by decoded exact values, never by golden bytes (Concordance 1.4).
- **Bindings for other languages** as a later phase, built on the
  conformance read and the wire specification, not on the Rust crate's
  internals. A first binding (for example Python) carries exact values
  only: integers of any size and rationals as numerator and denominator,
  never floats.

## 5. Not carried forward, and why

| Precursor practice | Why it is not carried forward | Mycelium's form |
|---|---|---|
| **Scored selection:** a candidate chosen by the minimum or maximum of a functional | A score decides what the algebra should derive, and its tie rule is hidden | Canonicalize and difference; a named order with its tie named (Concordance 1.10) |
| **Weighted votes:** agreement as a weighted or majority count of readings | Agreement is algebraic; nothing is outvoted | Second vantage, `same_vertex` (47.3) |
| **Digest identity:** an object identified by a hash of its encoding | Two encodings of one object, or one digest of two, decide equality wrongly | Identity by decoded coordinates; hashes compact coordinates held losslessly |
| **Float simulators:** physics or world dynamics in floating point crossing a boundary | Floating point is inexact and order-dependent | Exact ℚ; floats stay inside a kernel's own shell and never cross (MU-HOSTING §3.1) |
| **Golden-byte tests:** stored encodings compared byte for byte | Conformance is by decoded value (Concordance 1.4) | Decoded claims and round-trip identities |
| **Enumeration sieves:** candidate positions, rules or parameters listed and filtered | Search over what the algebra can read directly | Hermite and Smith forms, reduction, measured holes |
| **Quorum constants:** a fixed count of agreeing parties as a threshold | A constant where the law names a vantage | Two independent bonded vantages, by rule |
| **Log scraping:** invariants read from text output | Text is not a typed value | Typed readings (`observe`), decoded ledger rows |

## 6. convergence-engine

convergence-engine ([github.com/SAIOS-SAMN/convergence-engine](https://github.com/SAIOS-SAMN/convergence-engine))
is the public prototype harness from which Mycelium grew. It is kept public
as a record and as a source; it is not maintained as a product.

Mycelium keeps its exact rational arithmetic (`num-rational` over
`num-bigint`, no floating point) and its coboundary reduction (the
decomposition of a relational difference into a coboundary and a
remainder, which Mycelium carries in `transducer::cech`). Components drawn
from it are listed above: the rational canonical form over ℚ[x] (§3.4) and
the refused functionals it is the source of (§3.11). Its scored coherence
functional, its convergence ratios over scalars and its digest identity of
similarity classes are not carried forward (§5).
