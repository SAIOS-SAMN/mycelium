## 1. What stands today

The geometry audit on #431 found 203 lattice and Euclidean sites past the sensor. Most rest on one construct, the unit-cube body on ℤⁿ:
- **The site** is `SectionSite([i64; ALPHABET_SAMPLE_RANK])`: two integers on the perspective's plane, with a third, the frame `k`, added by `on_family`.
- **The body** is a set of face-adjacent unit cubes: `blade::components` over `sentence::face_incidence`, "one coordinate differing by one". Contact is edge or corner adjacency (`shared_cells`, `named_incidence`).
- **The lift** gives each compartment "the unit cube's inversive extent, `1/12` along each axis" (`round_extent`). The centroid and inertia are read in absolute integer units.

The manifold the doctrine names already exists. `transducer::manifold` reads each axis as a circle `S¹` of its period, or an interval with boundary, and the sample as their product (`Manifold::lift_from`). Nothing forms a body on it.

## 2. Derivation

1. **The sample is its manifold** (78.179 §1). Each axis is a circle of period `p` where the content repeats `p` steps on, and an interval of extent `e` otherwise. The sample is their product: `Tⁿ` where every phase is periodic, a fibration where an axis is nested.
2. **A site is a point of the manifold**, never two integers. On a circle phase, the coordinate `s` is the turn `s/p` with its winding `⌊s/p⌋` kept beside it, never reduced mod 1 (MU-ANTI-FLATTENING 12; AGENTS drift table). On an interval phase it is the place `s/e` in `[0, 1)`, with the boundary held. The point has the sample's own rank: one coordinate per axis, as many axes as the sample has. It is not fixed at two.
3. **Neighbours are read on the manifold.** Two points are neighbours where they differ by one step of one phase's own resolution (`1/p` or `1/e`). On a circle, the far seam glues to the near one, so the last step and the first are neighbours. On an interval, the boundary is not crossed. The raster's adjacency stays the sensor's (law 11). What a body is made of past intake is the manifold's adjacency.
4. **A body is a connected region of the manifold, held by its boundary** (MU-ANTI-FLATTENING 12: "each body held by its boundary, incidences between boundary arcs"). The region is the connected component under step 3. Its boundary is the set of steps that leave it. Contact between two bodies is a shared boundary arc, an incidence, never a corner count.
5. **A body's extent is read in the manifold's own periods.** A point's cell on phase `k` spans one step, `1/p_k`, so its second moment there is `1/(12·p_k²)` in turns. That is the sample's own resolution (MU-ANTI-FLATTENING 12: "each frame at its own resolution"), never the absolute unit cube's `1/12`. Two samples of different extent, relating by the re-period of each phase (78.179 §1; MU-SENSING §3.1), then read the same body with the same moments.
6. **Identity is by position on the manifold.** A word's chain is placed by its points' turns and windings. On a torus there is no corner to translate to, so the least-corner identity (`residue::corner_of`, enforced in `court/verdict/blind.rs:255`) is retired.

## 4. Claims for S1 (written before the code)

1. A circle phase of period `p` reads site `s` as the turn `s/p` with winding `⌊s/p⌋`. An interval phase of extent `e` reads it as `s/e`, with no winding.
2. On a circle, the last step's point and the first step's point are neighbours. On an interval they are not.
3. A body painted across a circle's seam is one region of the manifold. On an interval the same paint is two regions.
4. The point has the sample's rank: a rank-3 sample's points have three coordinates.
5. No unit cube enters: the claims hold whatever the sample's extent, at every re-period of its phases.
