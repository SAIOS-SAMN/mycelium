## 1. The law, in one sentence

A field is a curved, nested manifold whose geometry is produced by the
orbits it holds. Its sites are held by phases within the charts of its
atlas, and the transitions between charts (versors of `Cl(n+1, 1)`) are
its connection. It curves into a well around each attractor whose local
orbit it holds and is flat where nothing is held. A membrane encloses it
where it encloses. Each loop is read by the holonomy of its
transitions, in turns, and its open readings are held as a vortex.
Nothing in it is a scalar except the gauge of a one-dimensional reading.
No round reference (circle, sphere, torus) is part of it.

## 2. The carrier

| Part | Held as | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Rank | `n`, read from the data | A constant |
| A chart | A body's exact invariant coordinates, its shape class, coarsest first; the field's charts form its atlas (MU-RECURSION 78.41) | A round reference (circle, sphere, torus) |
| A transition | The versor of `Cl(n+1, 1)` that carries one chart onto another; the transitions are the field's connection | A coordinate change on a fixed surface |
| A point | `n` phases within a chart, each a turn in ℚ/ℤ of that chart's period (reduced fraction), held projectively: the common scale is a gauge | A unit vector, a squared norm, a float radius, `e∞` outside an enclosure |
| A well | The curvature around one attractor, produced by its local orbit; a held percept and a held thought are one well at two stages | A stored mass, a radius, a constant |
| Curvature | The holonomy of a loop of transitions, produced by the orbits held: the shear of the local orbits on the orthogonal orbit class (gravity); flat where nothing is held | A metric fixed in advance, a scalar curvature |
| The membrane | The field's boundary where it encloses; the infinity is enclosed there | `e∞` standing outside an enclosure |
| Nesting | A level per factored phase, each level a field within the field (MU-SENSING §3.6) | A flat product |
| A motion | A versor of `Cl(n+1, 1)`: a rotor by turns (exact over `ℚ(ζ_ℓ)`), a reflection or an inversion in the membrane, a boost; or a re-period of one phase | A Euclidean transform, a matrix of floats |
| A loop | A loop of charts, with the transition at each step | A sequence of coordinates; a polygon on a reference surface |
| A vortex | The candidates over one demand, each a section of the gauge fibre (MU-RECURSION 25.12) | One chosen candidate |

## 3. The readings

1. **Holonomy:** the product of the transitions around a loop, read by
   its modes: an exact turn in ℚ/ℤ where elliptic, with its order a
   winding in ℤ (MU-RECURSION 10.3, 12.6).
2. **Monodromy of a candidate:** flat (order 1), torsion (order `ℓ`) or
   rupture (infinite order), read in `Spin(n+1, 1)`.
3. **An orbit:** a base loop and a fibre winding (ℤ). Its precession per
   lap is the base loop's holonomy. An orbit with zero precession is
   refused as a loop.
4. **Axiomatic gravity:** the local orbit's shear on the orthogonal orbit
   class, read from the orbits held.

## 4. The refusals

| Refusal | When |
|---|---|
| `NotExact` | A motion or turn the carrier cannot hold exactly; the residue is named |
| `Loop` | An orbit with zero precession, returning exactly |
| `Unenclosed` | A point, motion or reading that needs infinity outside the membrane |
| `ScalarCollapse` | A reading that collapses a multivector to one number other than the gauge of a one-dimensional reading |

## 5. The wire

- The rank `n`.
- For each point: the `n` phases as reduced fractions in the rational lane
  codec (MU-ECONOMY §1), then the nesting levels.
- A loop: its charts and transitions in order, each transition a versor
  over its cyclotomic field.
- A reading: its typed value (a turn, a winding, an order).
- Conformance is by decoded exact values (Concordance 1.4).

## 6. Joint-read fixtures (to be agreed)

Each fixture is an input and its exact decoded output. Every kernel must
reach the same objects:

1. A loop of three charts whose transitions are rotors by `1/12` turn
   each, in one plane: the product is a rotor by `¼` turn, so the
   holonomy is `¼` turn, of order 4.
2. A loop in a flat region of the field: holonomy `0`, so the orbit is
   refused `Loop`.
3. A rotation by `1/5` turn composed five times: the identity, exact over
   `ℚ(ζ_5)`.
4. A phase of period 9 taken apart as `3 · 3`: site 7 maps to `(2/3, 1/3)`.
5. Two candidates over one demand with monodromy orders 1 and 3: one flat,
   one torsion with period 3.
6. A point needing `e∞`: refused `Unenclosed`.
